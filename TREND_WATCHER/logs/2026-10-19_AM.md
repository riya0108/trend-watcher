### Full debate piece draft — same fuel, different pump prices

**Format:** Instagram carousel (side-by-side price comparison + a
two-sided cost question suits carousel over Reel).
**Story broke:** Oct 11, 2026 (private-retailer hikes); ongoing as of
Oct 19, 2026.
**Publish-by deadline:** Oct 22-23, 2026 (working target, not a hard
external date).

**Truth layer:**
- **FACT:** Jio-BP raised petrol prices by Rs 5/litre and diesel by Rs
  3/litre effective Oct 11, 2026, across its outlets; Nayara Energy had
  separately raised prices across roughly 7,000 outlets earlier in
  October. State-run oil marketing companies — Indian Oil, BPCL, and HPCL
  — have kept their regular retail petrol and diesel prices unchanged
  this month, as of the most recent reporting found (Oct 13, 2026).
  Brent crude has been trading above $105-108 a barrel through October,
  its highest levels in months, amid escalating attacks on Saudi energy
  and other Middle East infrastructure. India imports roughly 90% of its
  crude oil requirements. Reported national-average and city-level pump
  prices as of mid-October: national average petrol ~₹111.21/litre,
  diesel ~₹97.83/litre; Delhi ~₹102.12 petrol / ₹95.20 diesel (Oct 13).
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** None found this pass — no named company official
  (Jio-BP, Nayara, or any PSU oil company) or government spokesperson was
  quoted in this pass's sources explaining *why* PSU prices are being
  held while private prices rose. Deliberately left blank rather than
  invented; see UNKNOWN below.
- **INTERPRETATION:** Private retailers, which do not carry the same
  government price-support arrangements as the PSU oil marketing
  companies, appear to be passing rising crude costs through to pump
  prices directly, while the state-run companies appear to be absorbing
  the margin impact rather than raising prices this month.
- **OPINION:** (left for the human writer — e.g., "PSUs shielding
  consumers from a war-driven spike is the system working as intended"
  vs. "propping up below-cost PSU pricing just delays the pain and
  distorts competition with private players facing the identical input
  cost" — not asserted by this agent.)
- **PREDICTION:** Whether/when the state-run companies will raise prices
  to catch up is not established by anything found this pass — flagged
  as unknown, not predicted.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether PSU prices are being held due to explicit
  government direction (e.g., festive-season or political
  considerations), independent commercial decisions by the PSUs
  themselves, or something else; the precise margin loss PSU companies
  are currently absorbing; whether/when a catch-up hike comes.

**The debate:**
- **Side A (bull / PSUs holding the line is right):** Shielding ordinary
  consumers from a crude-price spike driven by a Middle East conflict
  nobody in India caused is sound social policy, especially during the
  festive season — and PSU oil companies, backed by the state, are
  better placed to absorb a temporary hit than ordinary households are.
- **Side B (bear / this is an unsustainable distortion):** Holding PSU
  pump prices below actual cost doesn't make the crude bill disappear —
  it just defers it, while putting private retailers facing the identical
  input cost at an artificial disadvantage; eventually either consumers
  get a sharper catch-up hike, or the cost resurfaces as PSU losses paid
  for some other way.
- Why both sides are real: the same underlying fact — PSU prices frozen
  while private prices moved — genuinely supports either reading; neither
  side is obviously wrong without knowing *why* the PSUs are holding (see
  UNKNOWN).

**So-what:** Right now, which brand of pump a vehicle owner happens to
drive into determines what they pay for the same fuel — a concrete,
checkable, pocket-level fact that most people assume isn't true (that
petrol prices are uniform within a city), tied directly to a live global
oil shock.

**Structure sketch:**
1. Hook: "Two pumps, same street, same petrol — different price. Here's
   why."
2. Facts: the October hikes, which companies moved and which didn't, the
   crude backdrop
3. Side A: why holding PSU prices steady is the right call
4. Side B: why it's an unsustainable distortion
5. So-what: what to actually check next time you fill up
6. CTA: "Have you noticed the price gap at your local pumps? Drop your
   city."
