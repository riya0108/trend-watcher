## Candidate 1: NSE's IPO — is India's most-hyped listing also its most overpriced?

- First seen: Google News / Business Standard / CNBC coverage throughout
  today (CNBC piece "Investors rush into India's NSE IPO at valuation
  multiple above Nasdaq" published today, 2026-09-22); valuation-debate
  pieces ("Pricey affair! Is NSE's valuation premium to global peers
  justified?") have been running in Business Standard since ~Sept 16.
- Story broke (best estimate): Allotment for the NSE IPO was finalised
  today, Sept 22 (exact hour unconfirmed by sources found — typically
  mid-morning IST for registrar finalisation). Listing is set for Sept 24
  on the BSE; shares credit to demat accounts Sept 23.
- Raw framing: "NSE IPO allotment finalised; issue was 5.7x subscribed."
- Reframed hook: "Millions of Indians just found out today whether they
  got shares in the NSE IPO — but top analysts warn they may be overpaying
  for the world's most expensive stock exchange stock, priced richer than
  Nasdaq."
- **Score:**
  - 🔥 Trend: 1 — top financial story across Indian business press today;
    allotment-result day for a heavily-hyped ₹22,562 crore ($2.3B) IPO.
  - 😮 Surprise: 1 — NSE trading at a richer earnings multiple than Nasdaq
    or Intercontinental Exchange is a genuinely counter-intuitive stat for
    a general audience.
  - 💰 Impact: 1 — real money for the ~20.4 lakh applicants (per
    BusinessToday) who bid, deciding now whether to hold through listing
    or sell.
  - ⚔️ Conflict: 1 — real, named disagreement: brokerages Swastika,
    Geojit and GEPL Capital rate it "Subscribe" at ~42-43x FY26 EPS,

    Rahul Sharma (Equity99) calls the same 42.8-42.9x multiple "expensive,"
    particularly after a reported decline in NSE's FY26 PAT.
  - 🎥 Visual: 1 — valuation-multiple comparison chart (NSE vs Nasdaq vs
    ICE vs BSE), subscription-numbers graphic, listing-day countdown.
  - **Total: 5/5**
- **Still early?** Yes, relative to the event that matters most — listing
  day (Sept 24) hasn't happened yet, so the "was this a fair price" debate
  is still live. The narrower "did you get shares" trigger, though, broke
  earlier today, so that specific reaction window is already tight by this
  evening pass.
- **Verdict:** PROCEED (5/5).
- First-reaction deadline: allotment news broke ~this morning IST, so the
  strict 2-4h window has likely already closed by 18:00 IST — noted below,
  drafted anyway as a listing-day-countdown angle rather than a pure

- Full debate piece: flagged YES — enough named positions on both sides

## Candidate 2: UPI's "free forever" myth meets its first real fee

- First seen: NPCI's MDR announcement broke ~Sept 17-18 (Business
  Standard: "UPI MDR returns... merchants, retailers, petrol pumps,
  banks"). Today, Sept 22, NPCI issued a clarification (BusinessToday
  headline dated today: "NPCI says small merchants and 96% of transactions
  remain unaffected from October 15") and the Ministry of Finance
  separately reiterated no GST applies on UPI transactions — both read as
  a direct response to several days of public pushback.
- Story broke (best estimate): ~Sept 17-18 for the original MDR
  announcement; today's clarification is a fresh escalation, not a new
  story.
- Raw framing: "NPCI sets 0.4% MDR on UPI merchant transactions above
  ₹2,000, effective Oct 15."
- Reframed hook: "UPI was sold to India as 'free forever' — starting
  Oct 15 a real fee kicks in for some transactions, and today NPCI is
  scrambling to reassure users after petrol pumps, stockbrokers and
  politicians pushed back."
- **Score:**
  - 🔥 Trend: 1 — multi-day developing story with a fresh clarification
    dropping today across Business Standard / BusinessToday / Tribune.
  - 😮 Surprise: 1 — breaks a widely-held "UPI is completely free"
    assumption, even though the fee only touches a slice of transactions.
  - 💰 Impact: 1 — directly hits merchant margins (petrol pumps flagged
    thin margins) and reshapes public trust in a payment rail nearly every
    reader uses daily.
  - ⚔️ Conflict: 1 — NPCI / payments industry vs. petrol-pump dealer
    associations (who warned they may revert to cash above ₹2,000),
    stockbrokers (concerned about large capital-market transactions), and
    reported political criticism.
  - 🎥 Visual: 1 — tiered "who pays what" breakdown (P2P free, <₹2,000
    free, >₹2,000 at 0.4% capped at ₹300, small merchants exempt).
  - **Total: 5/5**
- **Still early?** No — the underlying story is ~4-5 days old and has
  already run in multiple major outlets (Business Standard, The Wire, Al
  Jazeera). Already saturated.
- **Verdict:** PROCEED (5/5) — per PROCESS.md §2C, saturated stories only
  pursue Stage 4, and only with a genuinely fresh angle. That angle here:
  today's NPCI "96% unaffected" clarification plus the Finance Ministry's
  separate no-GST-on-UPI statement, pulled into one explainer that lays
  out exactly who is and isn't affected — most single-outlet coverage so
  far has only covered one side of this.
- First-reaction deadline: window already closed (story broke days ago);
  draft below is for reference / as a possible explainer opening line, not
  a live "breaking" post.
- Full debate piece: flagged YES — named parties on both sides, concrete
  numbers, real unresolved friction. Best published now, before the
  Oct 15 effective date makes it moot.

## Candidate 3: GST 2.0 turns one — and the states-vs-Centre numbers don't agree

- First seen: Anniversary-date coverage across Indian business press
  today, Sept 22 — exactly one year since GST 2.0 took effect
  (2025-09-22). Business Standard: "One year of GST 2.0: Net GST revenue
  shows signs of recovery."
- Story broke (best estimate): This is a scheduled anniversary, not a
  breaking event — "broke" today by calendar, coverage same-day across
  outlets by design.
- Raw framing: "GST 2.0 marks first anniversary; net revenue recovering."
- Reframed hook: "One year after GST 2.0 made your soap, toothpaste and
  milk cheaper, state governments say they're the ones footing the bill —
  but SBI's own research says states are actually up ₹1.43 lakh crore.
  Both numbers can't be right."
- **Score:**
  - 🔥 Trend: 0 — calendar-driven anniversary coverage, not an organic
    spike; not "everyone suddenly talking about it."
  - 😮 Surprise: 1 — the two competing estimates (a ₹1.43 lakh crore
    *gain* vs. a ₹15,000-20,000 crore *annual loss*) are wide enough apart
    to genuinely surprise a reader that anyone can be this uncertain a
    year in.
  - 💰 Impact: 1 — real for consumers (cheaper household goods, nil-rate
    items like UHT milk and Indian breads) and for state government
    budgets/services.
  - ⚔️ Conflict: 1 — named: eight opposition-ruled states (Himachal
    Pradesh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Telangana,
    West Bengal), with Karnataka Revenue Minister Krishna Byre Gowda
    specifically pushing for the compensation question to be discussed at
    the GST Council, vs. SBI Research's gain estimate and a Centre that
    reportedly did not take up the compensation ask for discussion.
  - 🎥 Visual: 1 — before/after GST-slab rate chart, revenue-recovery line
    chart, state-by-state impact framing.
  - **Total: 4/5**
- **Still early?** No — anniversary pieces are, by construction, already
  the "5th+ outlet" covering it today. Saturated.
- **Verdict:** PROCEED (4/5) — Stage 4 only per §2C. Fresh angle: most
  anniversary pieces run either the "prices got cheaper" consumer angle or
  the "states are losing money" fiscal angle separately; putting the SBI
  Research number and the states' compensation demand in the same piece,
  named, is the angle not yet widely done today.
- First-reaction deadline: window already closed / not applicable to a
  scheduled anniversary; draft below is for reference only.
- Full debate piece: flagged YES — named states, a named minister, a named
  research house, concrete rupee figures on both sides.
