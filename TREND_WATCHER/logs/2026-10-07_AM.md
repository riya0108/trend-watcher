### 1. RBI just hiked rates for the first time since 2023 — was it the right call?

Format: carousel (suits "what happened → the case for the hike → the case
against it → what it costs/earns you now" structure well).
Story broke: this morning, Oct 7, 2026.
Publish-by deadline: within 24-48h — this is as fresh as the story gets;
publishing today or tomorrow is ideal.

**Truth layer (updated from the Oct 6 AM draft — prediction now resolved):**
- FACT: RBI's Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25bps to
  5.50% on Oct 7, 2026, and changed its policy stance to "calibrated
  tightening." This is RBI's first rate hike since February 2023. (Source:
  Upstox and Forbes India live-blog coverage of today's announcement.) The
  rate had stood at 5.25% since the committee's prior hold. India's retail
  inflation rose to 4.82% in August 2026 from 4.45% in July; Brent crude
  had risen from roughly $80/barrel to roughly $102/barrel ahead of the
  decision.
- ATTRIBUTED CLAIM: SBI Research had estimated (per reporting carried
  forward from Oct 2-6) that a 25bps hike raises the EMI on a ₹50 lakh,
  20-year home loan by roughly ₹750-850/month, and had called for a further
  hike in December; Nomura had put an 80% probability on a cumulative
  25-50bps of hikes this cycle. Per general reporting on today's
  announcement, RBI's stated reasoning centered on inflation risk, elevated
  crude prices, and currency/external pressure — this is a paraphrase of
  secondary reporting, not a verified direct quote from Governor Malhotra,
  and should be checked against RBI's own press release/statement before
  attributing specific language to him.
- INTERPRETATION: The shift from a "hold" consensus in late September to a
  confirmed hike on Oct 7 suggests the inflation and crude data released in
  the intervening weeks moved the MPC's own assessment, consistent with
  (not just the forecasters') analysis — a reading of the sequence of
  events, not a committee statement of intent.
- OPINION: Not pre-decided by this pass — the human writer should choose
  and label the editorial stance deliberately.
- PREDICTION: Whether RBI hikes again in December (as Nomura and SBI
  Research had flagged as likely) is unresolved and speculative.
- UNKNOWN: The MPC's exact vote count/split on this decision; the precise
  wording of Governor Malhotra's policy statement and press conference
  remarks; whether banks have yet passed the hike through to lending rates
  as of publish time.

**The debate:**
- Side A (the hike was the right call): inflation was visibly rising and
  crude was well above RBI's own planning assumptions; a hike now protects
  the rupee and household purchasing power before inflation becomes
  entrenched, and waiting would have meant acting from a weaker position —
  the same data-backed case SBI Research and Nomura made before the
  decision, now validated by the MPC's own action.
- Side B (the timing was wrong): the hike lands right as festive-season
  consumption (Flipkart Big Billion Days, Amazon Great Indian Festival,
  both starting this week) is meant to drive demand, and raises borrowing
  costs for home/car/business loans at exactly that moment; crude and
  inflation spikes can prove temporary, and a more patient RBI could have
  waited for confirmation the rise is structural before tightening.
- Why both sides are real: this isn't a strawman — credible institutions
  were on the hike side before the decision (SBI Research, Nomura, ~57-60%
  of a Reuters-polled economist panel), and RBI's own multi-meeting hold
  streak through 2025-26 is real-world evidence for the patience argument;
  commentary on both sides will be easy to find once market reaction
  settles today.

**So-what:** this changes, within days, what millions of Indians pay on
home, car, and personal loan EMIs, and what savers earn on fixed deposits —
right at the start of the festive shopping season.
