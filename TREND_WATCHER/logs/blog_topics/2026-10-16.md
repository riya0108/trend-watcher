## Topic 1: Why a "Guaranteed Returns" Telegram Stock Tip Just Cost Four People ₹55 Lakh

- **Category:** FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** Shape 2 ("Why do WE [universal behavior]?") crossed with Shape 4 (breaking

  everyone claims to know about still work) anchored to a specific, named, dated enforcement
  case.
- **The hook:** Everyone says they'd never fall for a stranger's stock tip on Telegram — yet
  SEBI just spent this week chasing down the money from four people who ran exactly that
  scheme. If the warning is this well-known, why does the scheme keep working?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This isn't an abstract fraud-awareness post — it's four
  named individuals with recovery notices dated this exact week (Oct 12–14, 2026), following
  on from a ₹55 lakh SEBI penalty order for manipulating a real, named stock (Moksh Ornaments
  Ltd) via misleading recommendations circulated on Telegram. It's concrete enough to carry
  the "here's exactly how the trick works, step by step" explainer the house style favors,
  with a genuine behavioral-psychology hook (why free tips promising guaranteed, fast money
  override the skepticism people claim to have) rather than generic "beware of scams" advice.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI penalty order: ₹55 lakh total imposed on four individuals — Amesh
  Surajlal Jaiswal (₹20 lakh), Jalaj Agrawal (₹15 lakh), Arvind Shukla and Aviral Saxena
  (₹10 lakh each) — for a social-media-based stock manipulation scheme involving misleading
  buy recommendations on Moksh Ornaments Ltd shares circulated through Telegram channels
  (reported by Moneylife). SEBI's enforcement listings show Notices of Demand (recovery
  certificates) issued against the same four individuals dated October 12–14, 2026: RC No.
  9301/2026 (Jaiswal, Oct 12), RC No. 9302/2026 (Shukla, Oct 13), RC No. 9303/2026 (Saxena,

## Topic 2: SEBI Just Made SME IPOs Easier to Buy Into and Harder to List On — Here's the Catch on Each Side

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** Shape 3 — dated India money explainer, numbers-heavy, directly actionable for
  anyone considering an SME IPO application this quarter.
- **The hook:** SEBI's new proposal cuts the minimum SME IPO ticket size in half and floats
  single-share trading — sounds like the door is opening wider for small investors. The same
  proposal roughly triples how much profit a company needs to list in the first place. Easier
  to buy in, harder to get in to buy — what does that trade-off actually mean for a retail
  investor eyeing one of these IPOs right now?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Released October 14, 2026 — two days before this pass,
  genuinely "happening right now" news, with the comment window open only until November 4,
  2026. SME IPOs have become a retail-investing fad in India over the past two years; this
  directly affects anyone currently weighing one, before most coverage has broken down both
  sides of the trade-off in one place rather than just the headline-friendly "ticket size cut"
  half of it.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI consultation paper dated October 14, 2026, proposing: minimum
  application size cut from ₹2 lakh to ₹1 lakh, with single-share trading lots under
  consideration; issuer profit test raised to an average annual operating profit of ₹3 crore
  over the preceding three years (up from ₹1 crore in two of the preceding three years);
  minimum allottees raised from 200 to 500; listing-eligibility criteria shifting focus from
  paid-up capital to market capitalisation. Comments open until November 4, 2026; nothing is
  in effect yet. Reported consistently by Moneylife, ETV Bharat, TaxGuru, Kotak Neo and
  Whalesbook (all October 14–15, 2026 datelines).
- **Angle source:** Same general India regulatory/markets scan as Topic 1
  (`BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5).
