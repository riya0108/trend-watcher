## Topic 1: Why Your Gold Jewellery Got More Expensive to "Make" This Diwali — Even as Indians Bought 29% Less of It

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" (consumer-psychology/economics explainer)
- **The hook:** Gold demand in India just fell to a multi-year low and prices have been
  sliding for weeks — so why didn't jewellery get any cheaper to have made? If anything,
  the fee jewellers pay just to get a piece hallmarked jumped 67% this year, and that
  cost quietly gets passed straight to the buyer, demand crash or not.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Every "gold price falls" headline this month implies
  jewellery should feel like better value. The making/hallmarking side of the bill moves
  on a completely different, government-set schedule that has nothing to do with demand
  — a genuinely counterintuitive mechanism most buyers never see broken out on their bill.
- **Real anchor:** World Gold Council's Gold Demand Trends (India Focus, Q3 2026): Indian
  gold demand fell 29% YoY to 162.5 tonnes (vs. 229.8 tonnes a year earlier); jewellery
  volume fell 20% to 99.9 tonnes even as jewellery *value* demand rose ~16%, and bar/coin
  demand fell 40%. Separately, mandatory hallmarking fees charged to jewellers by
  BIS-recognised Assaying and Hallmarking Centres rose from ₹45 to ₹75 per gold article,
  effective 14 September 2026 (reported by Business Today, 3 Nov 2026) — a cost jewellers
  can and do pass on to buyers.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan / WebSearch on India gold market data (Section 5,
  BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md) — not from bullorbear.in itself, no post like this found in the
  recent-logs duplicate check.

## Topic 2: Why the "Open-Source AI" Your Favourite Indian Founder Brags About Using Is Probably Chinese

- **Category:** BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" (reframed as a tech-behaviour
  "why" rather than a consumer-product friction, same mechanism-reveal shape)
- **The hook:** Half the Indian founders and freelancers posting "we ditched ChatGPT for
  a cheaper open-source model" on LinkedIn/X frame it as a scrappy, India-vs-Big-Tech
  win. A new industry survey says that "open-source" model is, 9 times out of 10,
  Chinese — and the reason it's cheaper isn't scrappiness, it's a price war between
  Chinese AI labs that most buyers never clock.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** "Open source" reads as neutral, maybe even
  American-adjacent, to most non-technical readers — the actual market is Kimi, DeepSeek,
  GLM and Qwen, all Chinese. That's a live example of the Raj Shamani/Vaibhav Sisinty
  framing lens this pass was meant to apply (money psychology + AI-tool behaviour), even
  though neither account had a dated November post to cite directly.
- **Real anchor:** Menlo Ventures' "2026 State of Enterprise AI" report (published 3 Nov
  2026, survey of ~400 US enterprise AI buyers): 89% of enterprise spending on
  open-source AI models goes to Chinese models — Moonshot's Kimi 61%, DeepSeek 15%,
  Zhipu's GLM 11%, Alibaba's Qwen 3% — versus Meta's Llama at just 7%, the top Western
  open-source model. Menlo also found the average open-source token price fell 3.3x to
  $0.13/million by September 2026, against a blended $0.70/million for closed models —
  the actual reason "open source" reads as cheap. (Reported consistently across SCMP,
  KuCoin News, BigGo Finance and the AI Matters newsletter; treating the Staying Ahead
  community itself as a lead rather than citable, per BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §4 —
