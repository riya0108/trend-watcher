## Topic 1: Why Your Favourite FMCG Brands Would Rather Shrink the Pack Than Raise the Price

- **Category:** BUSINESS (also sits comfortably under LIFESTYLE as a
  consumer-friction piece)
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" (everyday
  consumer annoyance reframed as a hidden-mechanism explainer)
- **The hook:** You didn't imagine it — the ₹10 chips pack feels lighter
  and the ₹30 Maggi pack cooks up thinner than it used to, but the price on
  the shelf never moved. Why do companies treat a smaller pack as invisible
  when a bigger price tag isn't?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Festive-season Q2 FY27 earnings
  commentary this month is full of companies explicitly choosing between
  "absorb the cost" and "raise the price" — making November 2026 exactly
  when the shrink-the-pack alternative (which most readers have never
  consciously connected to a pricing *strategy*, let alone one brands avoid
  naming) is actively playing out across grocery aisles. It's a classic
  Bull or Bear move: take the flat fact (quantities shrinking) and reframe
  it around the reader's own blind spot — why a gram cut registers less
  than a rupee hike, even though it's the same hit to the wallet.
- **Real anchor:** Nestlé India cut Maggi noodle pack sizes by up to 9%
  while holding retail prices unchanged (the ₹30 pack went from 150g to
  140g, the ₹60 pack from 300g to 280g — reported July 2026, company cited
  palm oil and packaging input costs). Layered on top: November 2026
  earnings-season reporting that roughly 15 Indian consumer-facing
  companies are holding back on outright price hikes to protect festive
  demand (Nestlé leaning on "operational efficiencies" instead), while
  Dabur and HUL have separately flagged "calibrated" price increases going
  forward as input-cost inflation persists (reported Nov 9 and Nov 11,
  2026).
- **Angle source:** General trend scan — India price-hike/FMCG/shrinkflation
  discovery queries (`BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5).
