## Topic 1: Why the RBI Just Burned Through a Record Chunk of Reserves to Defend a Currency You Don't Even Hold

- **Category:** FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** #4, India news/scandal explained (framed through #3's "why
  does this affect you" money-explainer lens)
- **The hook:** You don't own dollars or rupee futures — so why should it
  matter to you that India's foreign exchange reserves just had their worst
  month in years? Because every dollar the RBI spends defending the rupee
  is a dollar that would otherwise have kept your petrol, your imported
  phone, or your kid's semester abroad from getting pricier.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This has been building silently over
  four straight weekly declines (reported through the week ended Oct 2,
  2026) and is still live news as of Oct 10 — RBI just rolled out a special
  dollar window for oil companies and a new Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve
  requirement on forex derivatives to manage the pressure. It's exactly the
  kind of macro story nobody explains in reader-stakes terms before the
  effects (costlier imports, EMI/rate knock-on, a possible future rate
  move) show up on their own statements.
- **Real anchor:** Business Standard (Oct 9, 2026): India's forex reserves
  fell for a fourth consecutive week, down ~$12.95bn to $734.6bn as of the
  week ended Oct 2, 2026. A separate record single-week decline of ~$18.3bn
  was reported for late September, with reserves down roughly $51bn
  cumulatively from the all-time high of $785.7bn hit in the week ended
  Sept 4, 2026. BusinessToday (Oct 10, 2026): RBI introduced a special
  dollar window for oil firms and tighter Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve
  (FERR) rules for forex derivative contracts above $2m notional, tied to
  rupee defense amid high crude prices. (Note for the writer: exact weekly
  figures vary slightly by outlet — cross-check the RBI's own weekly
  statistical supplement before publishing a specific number.)
