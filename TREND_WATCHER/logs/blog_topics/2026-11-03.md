## Topic 1: Why Nobody Has to Tell You If Your Butter Paneer Is Actually Paneer

- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" consumer-psychology explainer (same lane as the archive's "Why Indian Brands Often Look Cheaper Than Foreign Brands" and the 2026-11-01 Biscoff piece, but a different underlying mechanism: substitution, not reformulation).
- **The hook:** Every time you've ordered paneer tikka or palak paneer at a restaurant, you've assumed it was made from milk solids. For a lot of dishes, it may actually be a cheaper non-dairy "analogue" product engineered to look and partly taste like paneer — and until very recently, no law anywhere required the restaurant to say so.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This has been building in slow motion since April 2025 (when the Consumer Affairs Ministry first floated labelling guidelines) but the enforcement side only caught up in 2026: FSSAI issued a public notice on correct labelling of cheese/paneer analogues in April 2026, and — more unusually — the National Human Rights Commission issued notices to all states/UTs and FSSAI over the issue in August 2026, framing mislabelled food as a rights issue, not just a food-safety one. That escalation (a human-rights body getting involved in a menu-labelling dispute) hasn't been covered as a plain-language "why does this affect you at the table" piece.
- **Real anchor:** FSSAI Public Notice on correct labelling of cheese/paneer analogues, reported via PIB on 29 April 2026; NHRC notices to all states/UTs and FSSAI on the analogue-paneer labelling issue, reported 15 August 2026 (PTI, via Telangana Today); a separate, earlier state-level order (reported as a Maharashtra government mandate requiring hotels/caterers to disclose non-dairy "analog" use) exists but its exact year was inconsistent across sources this pass — **verify the Maharashtra order's year directly against a state gazette/notification before citing it in the draft**, and lean on the FSSAI/NHRC dates (which are solid) as the primary anchor instead.

## Topic 2: Why That "Stock Tips" WhatsApp Group Suddenly Said It Was Shutting Down — Right Before Inviting You to a New One

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / FINANCE
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why do WE fall for this" reader-psychology piece, hung on a dated regulatory peg (SEBI naming the exact manipulation tactic, not just warning "scams exist").
- **The hook:** If you've ever been added to an "exclusive" stock-tips WhatsApp group, SEBI says you've probably seen this exact script: the group suddenly announces it's closing down — and within minutes, someone invites you to a "replacement" group. SEBI has now confirmed, officially, that this isn't a coincidence. It's a manufactured-urgency tactic with a name.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** SEBI's Press Release No. 65/2026 (22 October 2026) is unusually specific — it doesn't just say "beware of WhatsApp stock tips," it names the "group closes, then a replacement group opens" pattern as a deliberate astroturfing technique used by unregistered operators (often messaging from overseas numbers) to fabricate trust and urgency. NSE separately issued caution notices against five Telegram-channel operators running a similar scheme on 16 October 2026. This is a genuinely different psychological hook from the blog's 2026-10-16 "guaranteed returns" Telegram-scam post (which was about a promise-of-profit loss case) — this one is about why a fake scarcity trick works on people who'd never fall for an outright promise of guaranteed returns.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI Press Release No. 65/2026, dated 22 October 2026, cautioning investors against invitation-only WhatsApp groups and describing the "group closure → replacement group" astroturfing tactic; NSE caution notices against five entities/Telegram channels for unregistered stock-tip advisory, reported 16 October 2026 (Moneylife).
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (SEBI/regulatory news), per BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5.
