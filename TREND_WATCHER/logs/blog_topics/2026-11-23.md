## Topic 1: Why Milk Got More Expensive This Winter — the One Season It's Supposed to Get Cheaper

- **Category:** MONEY (also fits LIFESTYLE / PERSONAL FINANCE as a
  household-budget angle)
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" consumer
  explainer, with a dated report as the trigger.
- **The hook:** October to February is dairy's "flush season" in India —
  cows produce more milk after the monsoon, procurement costs ease, and
  prices are supposed to hold or soften. This winter, Amul and Mother Dairy
  both raised prices anyway, the second nationwide hike in six months. Why
  would milk get more expensive in the exact season it's built to get
  cheaper?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** A Mother Dairy price bump alone was
  considered and dropped from the 2026-11-20 pass for being "too thin and
  single-company, no two-sided angle beyond milk got costlier again." A
  CareEdge report published November 18, 2026 supplies the missing
  mechanism and the industry-wide data this pass needed: flush-season
  supply is unusually weak this year because of 15-20% higher cattle-feed
  costs (low/uneven rainfall hurting fodder) and thin skimmed-milk-powder
  inventories, so the seasonal rule that's supposed to protect consumers
  broke down. That's a genuine surprising mechanism, not just "prices went
  up again," and it's distinct from the 2026-11-15 shrinkflation post
  (that one was about pack size vs. price; this is about a seasonal pricing
  rule failing).
- **Real anchor:** CareEdge Edge Research report, published November 18,
  2026 (reported by Moneylife, Business Today, Daily Pioneer): retail milk
  prices up a cumulative ₹4-8/litre in FY27, with ₹2-6 of that added in the
  November round alone, "unusual" because October-February is normally the
  supply-rich flush season; cattle feed costs ~15-20% higher year-on-year
  as of September 2026; skimmed milk powder prices up ~36% year-on-year in
  October 2026. Separately confirmed: Amul and Mother Dairy both raised
  pouch milk prices by ₹2-4/litre effective November 12, 2026 (e.g. Amul
  Taaza toned milk ₹59 → ₹61), the second round of hikes in six months
  (first was in May 2026).
- **Angle source:** General consumer-price trend scan
  (`BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5), following up on a candidate the
  2026-11-20 pass flagged as real but not yet post-worthy.

## Topic 2: Why You Keep Ordering the Same Thing Even When You're Not Sure It's Still Your Favourite

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** Shape 2 — "why do WE [universal behaviour]?" — turns the
  lens on the reader's own psychology, matching the style guide's "Why We
  Compare Ourselves Constantly" vein.
- **The hook:** You open Swiggy, Zomato, or a shop's shelf and your hand
  goes to the same dish, the same brand, the same order — not because
  you've checked it's still what you'd actually pick, but because it's what
  you picked last time. New research says that's not just laziness: your
  brain may be weighting "I chose this before" as its own reason to choose
  it again, strong enough to override a genuinely better option sitting
  right next to it.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is a clean, citable "why do WE"
  piece with a real mechanism (not a flat "habits are powerful" platitude)
  — no "why we" piece has run in the last three weeks of logs, and this
  specific study hasn't been covered by any Indian outlet surfaced in this
  pass, so the Bull or Bear framing would be a first-mover reframe rather
  than a re-explainer of coverage that already exists.
- **Real anchor:** Wagner, B.J., Wolf, H.B. & Kiebel, S.J., "Action
  repetition biases choice in context-dependent decision-making,"
  *Communications Psychology* 3, article 177 (published online 26 November
  2025; TU Dresden press release 27 February 2026). Nine newly run
  decision-making tasks plus six reanalysed published datasets, 700+
  participants total; a hierarchical Bayesian reinforcement-learning model
  shows people's choices are biased toward actions they've simply repeated
  before — independent of the option's actual reward value — and that
  repeated options get rated as more preferred, even when an equal or
  better alternative is available in the new context.
- **Angle source:** General psychology/consumer-behaviour scan (standing
  in for the Raj Shamani / Vaibhav Sisinty lens this pass, since neither
  surfaced a fresh dated post — see dropped list).
