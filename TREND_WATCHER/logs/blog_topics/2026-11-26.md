## Topic 1: Why Your EMI Is About to Rise Again — and You Didn't Borrow a Single Extra Rupee

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen") crossed
  with Shape 3 (dated India money explainer) — the mechanism-reveal lens
  applied to a fresh macro release that lands directly on reader EMIs,
  FDs and SIPs.
- **The hook:** Nothing about your loan, your lender, or your credit score
  changed this month — but your EMI is still about to get more expensive,
  because a number almost nobody tracks just broke through the one ceiling
  the RBI set for itself.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** India's October retail inflation
  print, released November 12, 2026, hit 6.09% — above the RBI's own 6%
  upper tolerance limit for the first time in two years, beating the
  Reuters poll median of 5.93%. The RBI had already hiked the repo rate 25
  bps to 5.50% on October 7 (its first hike since February 2023, pre-dating
  this data), and economists (including ICRA's chief economist) are now
  pricing in a further 25 bps hike in December. That's a live, dated,
  two-step story — the rate move and the inflation data that justifies the
  next one — that directly explains why floating-rate home/personal/auto
  loan EMIs are about to reprice upward, which almost no reader will have
  connected to "onion prices."
- **Real anchor:** MoSPI's October 2026 CPI release (November 12, 2026):
  headline retail inflation at 6.09%, breaching the RBI's 6% upper
  tolerance band for the first time in two years; food inflation at 8.94%,
  driven partly by onion prices up over 100% y-o-y (reported by ThePrint,
  OneIndia, The Hans India, Down To Earth). RBI repo rate hike of 25 bps to
  5.50% on October 7, 2026 (its first since February 2023). Market
  expectation of a further 25 bps December hike to 5.75%, per a Reuters
  poll and ICRA commentary (reported by Finimize, 5paisa, ThePrint).

## Topic 2: Why You Can Picture Your Dream Life Perfectly and Still Can't Start Today

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** Shape 2 ("why do WE [universal behaviour]?") — turns the
  lens on the reader's own psychology, matching the style guide's "Why We
  Compare Ourselves Constantly" / fresh-start-effect family.
- **The hook:** Chronic procrastinators aren't vague about what they want
  — a new study finds they value their goals and can picture achieving
  them just as vividly as anyone else. What actually sets them apart is
  how much dread the thought of failing triggers, especially for anything
  due soon.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Directly undercuts the "procrastination
  = laziness or poor time management" cliché that most personal-productivity
  content still runs on, with a real, recent, peer-reviewed study rather than
  a listicle of tips — a genuine surprising-mechanism piece that fits the
  site's "full picture" tagline, and distinct from the already-published
  fresh-start-effect post ("Why We Wait for Monday to Change Our Lives").
- **Real anchor:** "High Trait Procrastination Predicts Increased Goal
  Anxiety Despite Invariance in Simulation of Goal Achievement" — J. Helgi
  Clayton McClure, Stephanie Sayan & Rachel J. Anderson (York St John
  University), published in *Psychological Reports*, 2026. The study found
  high-trait procrastinators rated their goals as equally meaningful and
  simulated achieving them just as vividly as low-trait procrastinators,
  but showed significantly higher anticipatory anxiety about goal failure
  — strongest for short-term goals — and were more likely to avoid their
  goals altogether (covered by PsyPost and Pulse, 2026).
- **Angle source:** Discovery query plan — psychology/consumer-behaviour
  beat (`why do people procrastinate psychology study 2026`, verified
  against the primary study via a second targeted search).
