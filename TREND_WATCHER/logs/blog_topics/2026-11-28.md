## Topic 1: Why Your Health Insurance Claim Keeps Getting Rejected for Reasons That Sound Made Up

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** #1 — everyday-friction "why does this happen" explainer
- **The hook:** You pay the premium every year, you disclose everything the
  form asks for, and then the one year you actually file a claim, it comes
  back rejected or half-paid — for a reason that was never explained to you
  upfront and that you can't find written anywhere in plain language.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is one of the most universal
  money-frustrations in India (almost every reader has either been through
  it or knows someone who has), but it rarely gets a proper "here's the
  actual mechanism" explainer — most coverage is either a listicle of "tips
  to avoid rejection" or a dry regulatory statistic. There's a genuine
  two-sided angle too: insurers have a real fraud/cost-control rationale for
  tight underwriting, but the categories reader experience as "wrongly
  rejected" is itself large and growing — a real tension, not a flat
  consumer-vs-company rant.
- **Real anchor:** Hansa Research's Customer Experience Score report
  (reported by Moneylife, August 2026) found 4 in 10 health-insurance
  claimants reported issues with their claim. Separately, a LocalCircles
  nationwide survey (reported by The South First, March 2026, covering
  claims filed 2023–2025) found 15% of claims were rejected and a further
  29% were only partially approved — both for reasons respondents considered
  invalid. (IRDAI's own last published annual-report figure, for context:
  11% of health claims rejected industry-wide, FY2024 data.)
- **Angle source:** Discovery query plan — personal-finance/insurance beat
  (`health insurance claim rejected report India 2026 survey`).

## Topic 2: Why You Still Walk Into the Store Even After You've Already Decided to Buy Online

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** #2 — "why do WE" reader-psychology piece
- **The hook:** You've compared prices, read the reviews, picked the exact
  product online — and then, instead of just clicking "buy," you drive to
  the store to look at it in person before paying. It isn't about the
  price. It's about something else entirely.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** "Webrooming" (research online, buy
  offline) is now the dominant cross-channel shopping habit, not a fringe
  one — bigger than its mirror-image "showrooming" — and the psychology
  behind it is counterintuitive: it's driven less by price-checking than by
  a documented emotional driver (anticipated regret) that nobody names when
  they do it. It's a clean fit for the site's "why do we" lane and hasn't
  been covered in any of the last two weeks' pitches.
- **Real anchor:** Salsify's "2026 Consumer Research" survey (nearly 3,000
  shoppers across the US, UK and Canada) found 67% of shoppers webroom,
  versus 53% who showroom (the reverse pattern) — webrooming has stayed
  flat year-on-year while showrooming has declined. Separately, peer-reviewed
  consumer-behaviour research (e.g. "Webrooming as the new retail
  experience: a smart-shopping perspective," International Journal of
  Retail & Distribution Management, vol. 51, issue 6) identifies anticipated
  regret — not price or convenience — as one of the strongest predictors of
  the habit. These are two independent, real sources cited separately, not
  a single conflated study.
- **Angle source:** Consumer-psychology discovery query
  (`consumer psychology study why people shopping behavior 2026`), cross-checked
  against the academic webrooming literature directly.
