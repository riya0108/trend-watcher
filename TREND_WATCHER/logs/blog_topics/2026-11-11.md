## Topic 1: Why the "60-Day Transformation" Photo That Talked You Into Booking That Clinic Might Have Been Entirely Fake

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen" consumer
  explainer) with a Shape 4 undertone (a new, named regulatory ban, dated
  and sourced).
- **The hook:** You've scrolled past dozens of clinic ads with dramatic
  before/after photos, tearful patient testimonials and "100% success
  rate" claims — for dermatology, dental, weight-loss and cosmetic
  procedures. India's medical regulator has now said, on the record, that
  it can no longer tell which of those were real patients and which were
  AI-fabricated — and has explicitly banned the fake ones.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Most readers assume a dodgy-looking
  testimonial is just bad marketing, not a defined, newly-illegal act. The
  National Medical Commission's ethical-advertising guidelines (issued
  Oct 6, 2026) are the first explicit India-wide ban on AI-generated
  patient testimonials, synthetic clinical outcomes and fake endorsements
  — a genuinely new mechanism most readers haven't seen named anywhere.
  It also lands right as winter/wedding season (peaks mid-Nov through
  Dec) drives the seasonal spike in exactly the cosmetic-dermatology,
  dental and weight-loss advertising this rule targets, so the reader is
  about to see a lot more of this content, not less.
- **Real anchor:** National Medical Commission ethical-advertising
  guidelines, issued Oct 6, 2026, effective immediately: explicitly bar
  doctors/hospitals from using AI to create "synthetic patient
  testimonials, voices, clinical outcomes or fake endorsements"; separately
  prohibit promotional before/after patient-outcome content (even with
  patient consent), unverified superiority claims ("best," "100% success"),
  and the use of celebrities/social-media influencers to promote medical
  services. AI-assisted content is allowed only if labelled as
  AI-generated. Enforcement sits with State Medical Councils, with
  escalating penalties (first violation: formal warning + mandatory ethics
  training). Reported consistently by PTI (via Deccan Chronicle),
  Careers360, Medical Dialogues, ThePrint and ocacademy.in — no reporting
  found of a November enforcement action yet, so the piece frames this as
  "the rule now exists and here's exactly what it bans," not "X clinic was
  just caught."
- **Angle source:** General India regulatory/consumer-protection scan
  (`BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5), via WebSearch. Not sourced from Raj
  Shamani / Vaibhav Sisinty or the Staying Ahead community this pass (see
  dropped list for why).

## Topic 2: Why the Government Just Forced Every Online Store in India to Show You Its Return Policy Before You Hit "Buy"

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE (could also sit under PERSONAL FINANCE / BUSINESS)
- **Pattern:** Shape 2 ("why do WE do this" — turns the lens on the
  reader's own behaviour: buying, wearing once, returning) crossed with
  Shape 4 (a new, dated government rule, explained).
- **The hook:** Millions of Indians bought a festive outfit this Navratri,
  wore it to one event, and mailed it back for a full refund — "wardrobing."
  A viral video of a delivery hub buried under returned Navratri clothing
  made the practice impossible to ignore in mid-October. What most people
  missed: the government's actual response to this pattern had already
  been quietly notified weeks earlier, and takes effect January 1, 2027.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** The viral moment made the *behaviour*
  visible, but almost no coverage connected it to the dated regulatory
  change already in motion — most readers have no idea this rule exists or
  that it was written with exactly this kind of return/refund murkiness in
  mind. It's also squarely in the post-Diwali return-and-refund window
  readers are living through right now, and distinct from the mutual-fund
  and bank-fee explainers that have dominated recent passes.
- **Real anchor:** Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules,
  2026 — Ministry of Consumer Affairs notification G.S.R. 789(E), dated
  Sept 9, 2026 (PIB press release Sept 10, 2026), effective Jan 1, 2027.
  Requires e-commerce marketplaces to clearly disclose return/refund
  policy, warranty terms, and delivery/payment details before purchase,
  alongside new disclosure duties on search-ranking criteria, sponsored
  listings, price-reduction claims and dark patterns; every e-commerce
  entity must join the National Consumer Helpline convergence process.
  Corroborated by the PIB release, LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, India Briefing,
  The Week and Inc42. Separately, a viral mid-October 2026 video (widely
  reported, e.g. Aaj Tak, Oct 18) showed a pile of returned Navratri/
  Dandiya outfits at what was reported as a delivery hub, sparking public
  debate over festive-wear "wardrobing" — the exact location and poster's
  claims are unverified per multiple reports, so the post treats the video
  as "a viral moment that sparked debate," not as a confirmed data point,
  and leans on the Gazette notification for every factual claim about the
  rule itself.
- **Angle source:** General India news/e-commerce-regulation scan
  (`BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5), via WebSearch.
