## Topic 1: Why FSSAI Just Took Nestlé to Court Over Your Baby's Formula

- **Category:** BUSINESS / POLITICS
- **Pattern:** India news/scandal explained (#4), framed with a "why does
  this happen" hook (#1) — same crossover style as the Sept 12 log's GST
  piece.
- **The hook:** Nestlé is one of the most trusted names on Indian shelves —
  yet the food regulator just filed three separate legal cases against it
  over infant formula, and the company's stock reportedly dipped on the
  news. What did FSSAI actually find, and why does a global brand's baby
  food keep ending up in this position?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is days-old, live news, not a
  retrospective — FSSAI initiated three adjudication cases against Nestlé
  India on/around 18 September 2026, reported by Business Standard, The
  Tribune, BusinessToday and Medical Dialogues. It's also a rare story that
  is simultaneously a consumer-trust scandal (new/expecting parents are the
  direct audience) and a market-moving event (shares reportedly reacted),
  which fits Bull or Bear's "full picture" tagline better than a pure
  health story or a pure stock story would alone.
- **Real anchor:** FSSAI's three adjudication cases against Nestlé India —
  covering NAN Excella Pro Stage 1, Lactogen Pro 1, and a follow-up formula
  — over (a) promotional claims ("5 HMOs," "Whey Protein," "easy to
  digest") alleged to violate Regulation 4(2) of the FSS (Foods for Infant
  Nutrition) Regulations, 2020 and Section 3 of the IMS Act, 1992, and (b)
  a lab finding that one follow-up-formula sample was substandard on biotin
  content. Reported 18-21 Sept 2026 (Business Standard, The Tribune,
  BusinessToday, Medical Dialogues, Organiser, Newskarnataka). Nestlé has
  stated its products are "fully compliant with all applicable
  regulations." Writer should pull the primary FSSAI notices/adjudication
  filings and Nestlé's official response directly before publishing, and
  flag clearly that adjudication is ongoing with no final penalty yet —
  don't imply guilt ahead of the ruling.

## Topic 2: Why Your Health Insurance Claim Gets Rejected Even When You Did Everything Right

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** "Why does [everyday friction] happen" (#1) — a mundane,
  widely-felt consumer annoyance reframed around the hidden mechanism, same
  family as the site's No-Cost EMI piece.
- **The hook:** You pay premiums for years, file a claim exactly by the
  book, and still get a rejection with a vague reason buried in the fine
  print. This isn't bad luck — it's happening to roughly half the people
  who file. Why is the system built this way?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Fresh regulator-level data (IRDAI's
  FY2024-25 annual report) plus a fresh consumer survey (LocalCircles) both
  landed recently and point the same direction — this isn't an old,
  well-worn "how insurance works" explainer, it's a documented, worsening
  trend. It's also timely: festive-season (Navratri/Diwali) is when a lot
  of readers buy or renew health and travel policies, so the friction is
  about to become concretely relevant to them.
- **Real anchor:** IRDAI's Annual Report FY2024-25 — insurers rejected
  roughly 8% of 3.26 crore health claims handled (about 1 in 12), with
  claims worth ~₹30,000 crore rejected or repudiated, a 15% jump over the
  prior year. Separately, a LocalCircles consumer survey found over half of
  respondents who filed a health claim in the past three years said it was
  rejected or partially approved for invalid reasons; Policybazaar's "Is
  India Happy with Health Insurance Claims? 2.0" study is a second
  consumer-side data point. Writer should pull the primary IRDAI report and
  the LocalCircles/Policybazaar survey documents directly rather than
  citing secondary aggregator summaries, and should independently verify
  any figure before publishing.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (WebSearch) — BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md
  §5.
