## Topic 1: Why We Keep Tapping "Always Allow" on Apps That Ask for Everything

- **Category:** BUSINESS / LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** "Why do WE [universal behavior]" (#2) — the AI-agent launch is
  the news trigger, the reader's own permission-granting habit is the actual
  story, same family as the site's existing pricing-psychology pieces.
- **The hook:** Meta's new personal AI agent, Muse, launched September 8 and
  expanded again on September 23 — it doesn't just chat, it asks to plug
  into six categories of your digital life at once: email, calendar,
  payments, health, shopping and smart home. It shot to #1 on the App Store
  and Play Store within weeks. Nobody sat down and read what they were
  agreeing to. A peer-reviewed 2026 study on exactly this behavior explains
  why — and it isn't really about Meta at all.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** It's a live, dated global-tech story
  (TechCrunch, Axios, Bloomberg, CNBC coverage from Sept 8 and Sept 23,
  2026) rather than a stale "AI is taking over" think-piece, and it pairs
  cleanly with a real, recent academic study on the exact mechanism —
  matching the site's habit of grounding psychology pieces in actual
  research rather than vibes. Muse itself is US-only for now, so the piece
  should be explicit that the *trigger* is global while the *behavior* is
  something Indian readers already do constantly — granting "always allow"
  to UPI autopay mandates, ride-hailing apps, health apps and AI assistants
  like ChatGPT/Gemini that ask for full inbox or calendar access. That's the
  "why does this affect you" pivot even though the named product hasn't
  launched here.
- **Real anchor:** (1) Meta's Muse — a personal AI agent built on the "Muse
  Spark" model, launched Sept 8, 2026 and expanded with new features
  (including a wearable) on Sept 23, 2026; reported to connect across six
  categories of user data (email, calendar, payments, health, shopping,
  smart home) and to have reached #1 on both the Apple App Store and Google
  Play Store shortly after launch (TechCrunch, Axios, Bloomberg, CNBC,
  about.fb.com's own announcement, Sept 8 and Sept 23, 2026 coverage).
  (2) "Towards Automating Data Access Permissions in AI Agents" (Wu et al.,
  accepted to IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2026, arXiv:2511.17959)
  — a controlled study of how people grant AI agents blanket "always share"
  data permissions; found the share of participants who *never* grant
  blanket "always share" access rose only from 17.2% to 25.6% even after
  they had directly observed the agent misusing data it didn't need for the
  task — i.e. the large majority keep granting blanket access even after
  seeing it go wrong. Writer should pull the paper (also indexed at
  computer.org/csdl and ResearchGate) and the primary Meta/TechCrunch
  coverage directly, and should not imply Muse is available in India — it
  currently is not.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (WebSearch) for the Meta Muse news
  hook; independent WebSearch for the academic anchor —
  BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5 and §2 (this is the kind of global-AI-story
  reframe §4/Staying Ahead is meant to surface, though it was found via

## Topic 2: Why Your EV Subsidy Takes Months to Actually Show Up

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** "Why does [everyday friction] happen" (#1), dated to a
  current India policy story (#3) — same family as the site's no-cost-EMI
  and FD-rate pieces: a system that looks straightforward on paper but
  isn't in practice.
- **The hook:** Delhi's EV Policy 2026 promises buyers their purchase
  incentive within 60 days of applying. In the first 83 days the scheme was
  open, 5,678 people applied — and by September 21, only 1,664 of them had
  actually received the money, around ₹4.80 crore paid out against
  thousands of pending claims. Nearly 71% of applicants
  are still waiting, well past the window the government itself set. And
  over 99% of these applicants aren't buying luxury EVs — they're
  two-wheeler buyers, the people for whom a delayed ₹5,000-15,000 subsidy
  actually matters to the monthly budget.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is fresh (reporting from Sept 21,
  syndicated through Sept 25, 2026) and squarely in the site's "why does
  the system work against the ordinary person" lane, but on a subsidy
  scheme rather than a bank product or corporate scandal — a genuinely
  different vein from anything in the last four passes. It also has real
  reader stakes: two-wheeler EV buyers are a mass-market segment, not a
  niche investor audience, which fits the "everyday friction" shape better
  than the site's more finance-specific pieces.
- **Real anchor:** The Tribune, "Delhi Govt sees 5,678 EV subsidy claims in
  83 days, 70% still await payouts" (reporting through Sept 21-25, 2026) —
  between July 1 and Sept 21, 2026, Delhi's Transport Department received
  5,678 applications for EV purchase incentives under Delhi EV Policy 2026;
  only 1,664 applications (about 29%) had resulted in payouts totalling
  ₹4.80 crore, while roughly 4,014 (about 71%) remained under scrutiny or
  processing; more than 99% of all applications were for electric
  two-wheelers, with 1,648 two-wheeler owners accounting for ₹4.61 crore of
  the ₹4.80 crore disbursed so far; the policy itself prescribes a 60-day
  disbursement window from application. Writer should pull the Transport
  Department's own portal data or an RTI-sourced figure directly rather
  than relying solely on the aggregator report, and should independently
  verify the current (post-Sept 21) figures before publishing since the
  numbers are moving week to week.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (WebSearch) — BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md
  §5.
