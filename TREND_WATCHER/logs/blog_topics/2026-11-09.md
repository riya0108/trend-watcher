## Topic 1: Why Your Recurring Payments Quietly Stopped Asking for Permission

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" (here, inverted: a friction *disappeared*, and that's the surprising part)
- **The hook:** You used to get an OTP every single time an app tried to auto-debit your account. Sometime this year, for most of your recurring payments, that stopped — and almost nobody noticed the safety net was gone.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** November is peak season for new recurring commitments — OTT renewals, festive-season EMI plans, insurance premium top-ups, SIP top-ups timed to bonuses. Readers are hitting the new no-OTP threshold for the first time right now, without realizing the rule under it changed months ago. It's a "wait, what?" piece hiding in plain sight, not a reaction to breaking news.
- **Real anchor:** RBI's Digital Payments – E-Mandate Framework, 2026 (circular RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396, effective April 21, 2026) removed the per-transaction OTP requirement for recurring auto-debits up to ₹15,000 — and up to ₹1,00,000 specifically for mutual fund SIPs, insurance premiums and credit-card bill payments. Only the *first* transaction under a new mandate still requires authentication; after that, the framework substitutes a mandatory pre-debit notification (24 hours ahead) and a post-debit confirmation in place of the OTP gate, and extends the RBI's existing unauthorised-transaction liability protections to e-mandate debits.
- **Angle source:** `BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` → general trend scan / `SOURCES.md` discovery query plan, RBI/banking beat. Corroborated across businesstoday.in, outlookbusiness.com, 5paisa.com and upstox.com coverage of the circular (note: these are secondary summaries — the circular number and effective date should be verified against rbi.org.in before publishing, since a couple of blogs disagreed on whether the ₹1 lakh exception extends to EMIs).

## Topic 2: Why You'll Happily Pay ₹600 for the Same Beer at a 5-Star Hotel That You'd Refuse to Pay ₹200 for Anywhere Else

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** Shape 2 — "why do WE [universal behavior]" — reader-psychology piece
- **The hook:** Same bottle, same brand, same alcohol content, same you — but your brain runs the price through a completely different mental ledger depending on where you bought it.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Diwali-season dining out, hotel brunches, wedding-season events and festive splurges are at their yearly peak right now, so readers are living this exact bias on repeat this month — a ready-made "oh, that's literally what I did last weekend" hook.
- **Real anchor:** "The Robustness of Mental Accounting Across 21 Countries," Journal of Consumer Research, 2026 (DOI 10.1093/jcr/ucag022) — a study of 5,000+ participants across 21 countries replicating the classic "beer on the beach" mental-accounting effect, confirming people pay significantly more for an identical product when it's bought in a higher-status context, independent of country or culture.
- **Angle source:** Dedicated psychology/behavioral-study WebSearch this pass; study flagged via jbs.cam.ac.uk's coverage, cross-referenced to its JCR/DOI listing. Fits the repo's "psychology pieces cite actual research" voice rule in `BLOG_TOPICS_STYLE.md`.
