## Topic 2: Why Some Shops Are Suddenly Nudging You Back Toward Cash After a Decade of "Just Scan the QR"

- **Category:** MONEY / BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" consumer explainer, with a dated news trigger
- **The hook:** UPI is still free for you, the customer — so why did your neighbourhood shop just start acting like it isn't?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** The "UPI stays free" headline was the easy version of this story; the merchant-side economics were not. NPCI's new merchant fee ends UPI's decade of being completely free for anyone in the chain, and it's exactly the kind of "flat fact → reader's lived experience" reframe this blog does well — the friction (a shopkeeper's hesitation at the QR code, a sudden "cash only for small amounts" sign) is something readers have plausibly already noticed without knowing why.
- **Real anchor:** NPCI introduced a 0.4% merchant discount rate (MDR) on person-to-merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000, effective 15 October 2026 — the first charge of any kind on UPI since merchant fees were zeroed out in January 2020. P2P transfers and P2M transactions up to ₹2,000 (reportedly >95% of UPI merchant volume) stay free; the 0.4% is split between issuing banks (40%), acquirers (30%), UPI apps (20%) and partner banks (10%). (Corroborated across SCC Online and Elets BFSI; NPCI cited the ~₹20,000 crore annual cost of running UPI as the rationale.) Note for the eventual draft: a third source's sector-specific flat-fee figures — e.g. ₹5 for railways/telecom/insurance — are single-sourced and should be verified against NPCI's own circular before being cited.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (financial/payments news), per BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5.
