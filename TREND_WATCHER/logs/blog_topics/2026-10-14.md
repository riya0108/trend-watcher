## Topic 1: Why a Celebrity Can Suddenly Front Your Mutual Fund's Ad — But Legally Can't Tell You Which Fund to Buy

- **Category:** FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** Shape 4 (breaking India news/regulation explained), with a Shape-1
  everyday-friction hook — the reader is about to start seeing recognisable faces in
  finance ads and won't know the fine print behind it.
- **The hook:** SEBI just let celebrities endorse brokers, mutual funds and investment
  advisers for the first time in years — but only at the brand level, never a specific
  scheme. What does that distinction actually protect the reader from, and what does it
  let slip through?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Notified October 8, 2026 — six days ago, right as
  the festive-season ad blitz (Amazon/Flipkart sales already live) ramps up marketing
  spend across categories including financial services. Readers will start seeing this
  within weeks, before most outlets have explained the mechanics.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI's Common Advertisement Code (new Chapter IIID, Regulations
  16G–16Q of the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008), notified October 8, 2026,
  covering stockbrokers, depository participants, investment advisers, research
  analysts, online bond platforms, portfolio managers and mutual funds/AMCs. Celebrities
  may endorse the entity/brand but ads "may not include any inducement or claim directly
  or indirectly regarding any specific product or service"; celebrity ads still need
  prior supervisory-body (or SEBI) approval, while most other ads move from
  pre-approval to post-issuance reporting within 3 working days. Board approved the
  framework September 24, 2026, after a June 23, 2026 consultation paper developed with
  AMFI. (Reported by Business Standard, TaxGuru, Storyboard18, Social Samosa, NewsBytes —
  cross-checked across multiple outlets.)
- **Angle source:** General trend scan per `BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5, standing in for
  the blog's own Finance/Business category pages since the live site was unreachable
  this pass.

## Topic 2: Why Your Bond Is About to Get a Traffic-Light Colour — and What Green Still Won't Tell You

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen") applied to a dated
  regulatory explainer — the friction being how opaque credit ratings on bonds/debt
  products currently are to a retail investor.
- **The hook:** Every bond and debt security a retail investor can buy is about to carry
  a mandatory six-level colour-coded risk label, from deep green to red. Sounds like it
  solves the "which bond is safe" problem — except it only measures one kind of risk,
  and readers need to know which one it leaves out.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Circular dated October 7, 2026 — one day before the
  ad-code news above, same regulator, same week. It's a concrete, numbers-and-mechanism
  piece that fits the blog's "dated India money explainer" lane and gives retail
  investors something actionable before the rule takes effect.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI circular dated October 7, 2026 making a colour-coded "Credit
  Risk-o-Meter" mandatory for debt securities — listed and proposed-to-be-listed
  non-convertible securities, commercial papers, securitised debt, security receipts and
  market-linked debentures, public or private placement. Credit ratings from AAA to D map
  to six colour-coded risk levels (ratings of B, C or D turn red); the meter reflects the
  lowest applicable rating; must be shown in offer documents, PPMs, ads and issuer/online
  bond platforms; must update within 24 hours of a rating change with no manual
  overrides; explicitly measures credit risk only, not market, liquidity or structural
  risk. Multiple outlets report a 45-day implementation runway, putting real-world
  rollout around late November 2026 (one outlet's claim of immediate October 7 visibility
  conflicts with this and is not used). (Reported by Moneylife, News on Air, NewKerala,
  Policy Edge, Angel One, CAalley — cross-checked across multiple outlets.)
- **Angle source:** Same general trend scan as Topic 1.
