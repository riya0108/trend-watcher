## Topic 1: Two Fake Court Orders Got 129 Betting Sites Blocked in India — and Nobody at the Telecom Ministry Checked

- **Category:** POLITICS
- **Pattern:** Shape 4 ("breaking India news/scandal, explained") — FACT-
  dense regulatory-failure story, explicit sourcing, two-sided (government
  overreach vs. forgery) exactly like the Rajesh Exports/SEBI or JPSC-JSSC
  posts in the style doc.
- **The hook:** The Department of Telecommunications ordered internet
  providers to block 129 betting websites this year — based on two High
  Court orders that, it turns out, never existed. Not "disputed." Not
  "under appeal." The courts themselves say they never issued them, and
  nobody at the ministry checked before pulling the trigger.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is still unfolding. A Reuters
  investigation published 11 November 2026 found the DoT acted on a fake
  Allahabad High Court order (dated 14 August 2026, forged under a sitting
  judge's name) to block 95 sites, and a separate fake Rajasthan High
  Court order to block 34 more in July. The Allahabad High Court filed a
  police complaint on 6 November after its own internal verification found
  no such order on record; UP Police have since registered an FIR against
  unidentified persons. The DoT reversed the Allahabad-order blocks around
  15 November — but the 34 sites blocked on the fake Rajasthan order were
  still blocked as of that reporting. The story is genuinely two-sided: a
  real public-interest goal (blocking illegal betting sites) undermined by
  a verification process so thin that forged documents sailed through it
  twice.
- **Real anchor:** Reuters' 11 November 2026 investigation; the Allahabad
  High Court's 6 November 2026 police complaint over the forged 14 August
  order; the UP Police FIR that followed; and the DoT's mid-November
  reversal restoring access to 95 of the 129 sites (corroborated by
  ThePrint, LiveLaw, Inc42, Deccan Chronicle, Moneylife, Lawbeat).
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (India legal/regulatory news) per
  BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5.

## Topic 2: Why Your Bank Won't Be Allowed to Call You About a Missed EMI Before 8 AM or After 7 PM Starting Next Year

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen") crossed
  with the dated-regulatory-explainer vein — same family as the 24-hour
  UPI-wait and bank-fraud-reporting posts: a friction everyone with a loan
  has felt, traced back to the rulebook that's about to change it.
- **The hook:** If you've ever gotten a loan-recovery call at 9 PM, or had
  a collections agent casually mention they'd "spoken to your office,"
  that's not just an aggressive bank — it's a gap in the rulebook that RBI
  has now closed, on a clock that starts 1 January 2027.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** RBI finalised its updated loan-
  recovery-conduct directions in 2026 (after reviewing feedback on a May
  2026 draft), consolidating and tightening the old 8 AM–7 PM calling
  window into a fuller framework: recovery calls must be logged and
  retained for at least six months, remote device-locking is barred
  outright except where the same lender actually financed that specific
  phone/tablet/laptop (and even then only after a 30-day grace period,
  with essential services like emergency calls kept running), agents
  can't lean on relatives, colleagues or social pressure to recover a
  debt, and borrowers get a path to compensation if a regulated entity
  breaks the rules. It takes effect 1 January 2027 — close enough that
  readers currently living with recovery calls should know what's about
  to change, and specific enough (exact call window, the 30-day device-
  lock grace period, the 6-month call-log retention) to be worth
  explaining properly rather than in a one-line news brief.
- **Real anchor:** RBI's 2026 directions on "Conduct of Regulated Entities
  in Recovery of Loans and Engagement of Recovery Agents," effective 1
  January 2027 — confirmed across The Federal, Exotel's summary, Business
  Today (7 Aug 2026), Moneylife, AnalyticsInsight and PolicyEdge, with the
  8 AM–7 PM calling-window figure independently corroborated against
  multiple outlets.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan / RBI regulatory tracking per
  BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5.
