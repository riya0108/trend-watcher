## Topic 1: Why Indian Parents Now Need Their Teenager's Sign-Off Before They Buy Anything

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen") crossed
  with the cross-cutting generational-commentary vein — the friction here
  is a reversal inside the household itself, not a company trick.
- **The hook:** If you're a parent, you've probably caught yourself asking
  your teenager "does this look okay?" before buying a washing machine or
  a phone — not just sneakers. If you're the teenager, you've noticed your
  opinion suddenly counts on purchases that have nothing to do with you.
  New research says this isn't a one-off quirk in your house; it's
  becoming the default pattern across urban India.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Most coverage of "Gen Z and money" is
  about Gen Z's own spending. This flips it — a Fireside Ventures study
  (covered by Tribune India/Newkerala in the past week) found Indian
  parents are increasingly validating their own big-ticket purchase
  decisions against what their Gen Z kids recommend, a reversal of the
  traditional parent-decides/kid-influences-only-lifestyle-categories
  model. It's a quiet shift in household power that most readers are
  living through without naming it.
- **Real anchor:** Fireside Ventures research on Indian household buying
  behaviour, as reported by Tribune India ("Indian parents now validating
  their buying decisions based on what their Gen Z teens recommend") and
  corroborated by Newkerala's coverage of the same report.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (Google-indexed India consumer-
  behaviour coverage) per BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5; thematically adjacent
  to the Raj Shamani money-mindset / India-audience framing lens.

## Topic 2: Why Your IndiGo Ticket Looks Cheap Right Up Until You Actually Need to Fly

- **Category:** BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen") — classic
  consumer-annoyance-as-hidden-mechanism, same family as "Why Flight Food
  Is So Expensive."
- **The hook:** You book a ₹4,000 IndiGo fare and still end up paying
  ₹6,000+ by the time you add a checked bag, a seat for your toddler, or
  just the right to board before everyone else — because the airline just
  quietly revised what each of those costs, again, with zero grace period.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Fare-tracking stories (surge pricing
  around weddings, festival travel) already got a pass on this blog in
  November. This is a different mechanism entirely: the *base fare* stays
  advertised-cheap while the airline's own published tariff for add-ons
  (excess baggage, infant travel, unaccompanied-minor service, "Fast
  Forward" priority boarding) keeps climbing — the real lever behind
  "cheap-looking flights," and most flyers never see the tariff sheet that
  changed.
- **Real anchor:** IndiGo's revised domestic ancillary-fee tariff, which
  took effect 17 September 2026 with no grace period — confirmed across
  multiple outlets (Wego's fare breakdown, Gulf News, Angel One, HDFC Sky,
  The CSR Journal, sahi.com), including the unaccompanied-minor fee rising
  from ₹5,000 to ₹5,999 and the travel-certificate fee from ₹200 to ₹300.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan / business-news search per
  BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5 (airline ancillary-fee coverage).
