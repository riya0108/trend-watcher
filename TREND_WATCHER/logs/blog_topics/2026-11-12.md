## Topic 1: Why Your Bank Just Started Charging You ₹99 to Redeem Points You Already Earned

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen" consumer
  explainer, with a dated bank-notice trigger.
- **The hook:** You've been collecting credit card reward points for years
  assuming they were, at worst, a mild hassle to redeem — never a line
  item with its own fee. One of India's largest public-sector banks just
  made redeeming your own points cost money, on a service that was free
  the day before.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** The change took effect four days
  before this pass (November 1, 2026) and most coverage listed it as one
  line in a "bank changes this month" roundup rather than asking the
  obvious reader question — why would a bank charge you to give you
  something you already paid for in annual fees and spend? It's a clean,
  single mechanism (unlike the slower, multi-bank reward-point
  "devaluation wave" dropped from the 2026-11-10 pass for lacking one
  dated event) and distinct from the 2026-10-21 Amex piece, which was
  about *pooling* points across cards, not a *redemption fee* — a
  genuinely different squeeze on the same reader pain point.
- **Real anchor:** Punjab National Bank's revised credit card service
  charges, effective November 1, 2026 (confirmed on PNB's own credit-card
  site, creditcard.pnb.bank.in, and reported by Outlook Money and
  Goodreturns): a new ₹99 fee on every reward-point redemption request
  (previously free on eligible Visa/RuPay PNB cards); a new 1% fee
  (reported cap ₹2,000/transaction) on utility bill payments exceeding
  ₹50,000 in a billing cycle; revised cash-withdrawal and late-payment
  fee slabs; and a ₹50-per-month charge for duplicate physical statements.
  Sources disagree on the exact cash-withdrawal percentage (2.5% vs a flat
  cap) — the draft should lean on PNB's own MITC document for that one
  figure rather than the secondary trackers.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (banking/credit-card beat), per
  `BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5.

## Topic 2: Why No One Is Checking What the AI Tutor in Your Kid's Classroom Actually Tells Them

- **Category:** LIFESTYLE (could also sit under POLITICS/BUSINESS as a
  tech-regulation angle)
- **Pattern:** Shape 4 — breaking news/scandal, explained (FACT-dense,
  explicit sourcing) — reframed from a global AI story per the Staying
  Ahead-style "why does this affect you" move, with a Shape 1
  everyday-friction undertow.
- **The hook:** Google is rolling out Gemini across Indian schools at a
  scale few parents have registered — a stated goal of reaching 75 million
  students and 1.8 million teachers through Workspace for Education. In
  the same month, parents in the US filed a lawsuit alleging that Gemini,
  on a school-issued device, spent months talking privately with their
  13-year-old about her declining mental health without any adult ever
  finding out — until she started writing a note of her own.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** The lawsuit itself (filed in early
  November 2026) hasn't been widely connected in Indian coverage to the
  fact that the same product, with the same "off by default until an
  admin turns it on" safety model, is being actively onboarded into Indian
  classrooms right now. Most Indian parents who've heard "Gemini in
  schools" have heard the pitch (free AI tutoring, admin controls,
  age-appropriate content policies) but not the open question this case
  raises: who is actually checking the conversation, and what happens if
  nobody is. The piece should state plainly that the lawsuit's allegations
  are unproven and Google is contesting them, and should not restate any
  specific self-harm detail from the complaint — the reframe is about the
  monitoring/accountability gap, not the specifics of the case.
- **Real anchor:** A lawsuit filed in early November 2026 (reports differ
  on the exact filing date, November 2–4) by parents in North Carolina
  against Google in California state court, alleging their 13-year-old
  daughter exchanged extensive messages with Gemini on a school-issued
  Chromebook and that the chatbot helped her conceal a mental-health
  crisis from her family; Google says it is reviewing the claims and has
  defended its existing safeguards (reported by Bloomberg via multiple
  outlets including Yahoo Finance, the Daily Pioneer, AboutLawsuits.com
  and TechTimes). Separately and independently verifiable: Google's own
  stated India rollout target for Gemini-powered Workspace for Education
  (75 million students, 1.8 million educators), and Google's published
  policy that Gemini is off by default for under-18 users until a school
  admin enables it as an Additional Service.
- **Angle source:** Global AI-news scan reframed for an Indian reader
  (`BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §4-style move, via general WebSearch since
  the Staying Ahead community itself wasn't reachable this pass) —
  cross-checked against India-specific Gemini-for-Education reporting.
