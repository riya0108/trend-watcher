## Topic 1: Why NPCI Pulled the Plug on a New UPI Fee Two Days Before It Was Due to Start

- **Category:** MONEY / BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** Shape 1 ("why does this everyday friction happen") fused with Shape 4
  (dated regulatory news, explained) — the friction being every UPI shopper's assumption
  that paying a shopkeeper by UPI is, and always will be, free.
- **The hook:** A fee on UPI payments to merchants was due to go live on October 15 —
  two days before it did, NPCI quietly shelved it "because of the festive season," with
  no new date given. The fee was never going to hit the person paying. So why did it
  need stopping at all, and who was actually about to pay for your "free" UPI habit?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This happened in the middle of the exact window
  readers are using UPI the most — Amazon's and Flipkart's festive sales are live, and
  Diwali shopping is ramping up. Most readers who use UPI daily have no idea a fee was
  ever scheduled, that it was deferred rather than cancelled, or that it will land on
  them anyway through merchant pricing the moment it returns post-Diwali.
- **Real anchor:** NPCI's UPI and Services Steering Committee deferred the planned 0.4%
  Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on person-to-merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000,
  announced October 13, 2026, two days before the fee's original October 15 start date;
  no revised date has been announced. The original structure: 0.4% MDR on P2M UPI
  payments over ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 or more, with a
  concessional flat ₹5 MDR for railways, telecom, insurance and fuel payments above
  ₹2,000. The fee is payable by merchants, not consumers directly; P2P transfers and
  merchant payments of ₹2,000 or less stay free either way. The Federal separately
  reported (citing sources, unconfirmed) that the deferral could run until January 1,
  2027. The Opposition publicly called the original plan a "UPI tax" that would burden
  consumers via pass-through pricing. Cross-checked across Khaleej Times, Angel One, The
  Print, Moneylife, The Federal, Manorama Yearbook, GKToday, Ventura Securities and
  Beebom (all dated October 13-14, 2026); core facts (deferral date, original fee
  structure, who pays) are consistent across all of them — only the unconfirmed revised
  date varies.
- **Angle source:** General India business/payments WebSearch sweep per
  `BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md` §5.

## Topic 2: Why the Government Just Sold Majority Control of IDBI Bank After Promising Parliament It Never Would

- **Category:** BUSINESS / POLITICS
- **Pattern:** Shape 4 — breaking India news, explained (FACT-dense, explicit sourcing,
  genuine two-sided conflict matching the site's "full picture before deciding which way
  to lean" tagline).
- **The hook:** The Cabinet just approved selling IDBI Bank's majority control to a
  Canadian insurance group for ₹52,878 crore — a deal the government itself once told
  Parliament it would never let happen, since it had committed to keeping at least 51%
  state ownership. Bank unions representing 90% of the sector's workforce are calling it
  "foreignisation," not just privatisation. The government says depositors and employees
  have nothing to worry about. Which part of that is actually checkable?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Approved October 14, 2026 — this week — after a
  process that started in 2022 and already failed once this year when bids came in below
  the government's reserve price. It's a live, unresolved conflict (union black-badge
  protests called for October 16) with a concrete, numbers-heavy mechanism most coverage
  is reporting as a headline deal size without explaining who actually ends up owning
  what, and what changes (or doesn't) for an existing IDBI account holder.
- **Real anchor:** The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved, on October 14,
  2026, the sale of a 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank to 10955230 Canada Inc. (a Fairfax
  Financial Holdings subsidiary) for ₹52,878.08 crore, at ₹81/share, followed by a
  mandatory open offer to other shareholders. The stake is split between the government
  (selling 30.48%) and LIC (selling 30.24%), leaving the government with 15% and LIC with
  19% (34% combined) after the deal — below the 51% state-ownership floor the government
  had previously told Parliament it would maintain. Completion still requires RBI and
  SEBI regulatory approval; a planned merger of Fairfax-controlled CSB Bank into IDBI
  within two years is part of the broader plan. The United Forum of Bank Unions (claiming
  90% of the banking workforce) and AIBEA/AIBOC publicly opposed the sale, calling it
  "privatisation" and "foreignisation" and calling a black-badge protest for October 16;
  the government's counter is that IDBI is already board-managed rather than a public
  sector bank, and that government+LIC retaining over 30% combined limits the practical
  change. Cross-checked across The Federal, Business Today, Moneylife, Swarajya, UNI
  India and Daily Pioneer (Oct 14-15, 2026 reporting), with the union protest confirmed
  separately by Business Today (Oct 15-16).
- **Angle source:** Same general India business/policy WebSearch sweep as Topic 1.
