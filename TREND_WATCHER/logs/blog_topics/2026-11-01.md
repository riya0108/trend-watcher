## Topic 1: The "Direct Plan Penalty" — How a Rule Built to Make Index Investing Cheaper Just Made It Pricier

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** Shape 3 — dated India money explainer (also carries Shape 1's "why does this friction happen" engine: the reader did everything right and still got charged more).
- **The hook:** Millions of Indians switched to direct mutual fund plans specifically to avoid paying a distributor's cut. A SEBI rule meant to make costs more transparent has, for some direct plans, made them more expensive instead — not cheaper.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** SEBI's (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 took effect April 1, 2026 and are still working through fund houses' books; an October 20, 2026 analysis (Aliceblue) and Outlook Money's "Direct Plan Penalty" piece are the freshest data points showing the real-world effect landing now, months after the rule passed — a classic "the fine print caught up with you" angle nobody's connected for a mass audience yet.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 — effective April 1, 2026 — replaced the single Total Expense Ratio (TER) with a split Base Expense Ratio (BER) + separately listed brokerage/statutory/regulatory levies; Outlook Money reports direct-plan TERs for some schemes rose between 0.01% and 1.20% over a two-month window as statutory levies that were previously absorbed got reclassified back into the chargeable total. (Primary rule: SEBI circular Dec 17, 2025 / effective Apr 1, 2026. Secondary reporting: Outlook Money "Direct Plan Penalty," 1finance.co.in TER/BER explainer, Aliceblue Oct 20, 2026 piece.)
