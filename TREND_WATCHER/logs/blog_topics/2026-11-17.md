## Topic 1: Why JustDial Couldn't Just Say "We're Only a Platform" Once Explosives Turned Up in Its Search Results

- **Category:** BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** 4 — India news/scandal, explained
- **The hook:** You've used JustDial (or IndiaMART, or any listings app) to find an
  electrician or a caterer and trusted the "verified/top" tag without a second thought.
  A government order just said the platform knew sellers were advertising a regulated
  explosive, took ad money from those listings anyway, and then tried the same legal
  defense every platform leans on — "we're just hosting what sellers post, we're not
  liable for it." The regulator said no.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is part of a dated, ongoing CCPA sweep
  (reported as recent as late October 2026) against seven e-commerce/listing platforms
  over unauthorised explosives listings — not a one-off. It's a precedent story: the
  reasoning the CCPA used against JustDial (safe harbour is conditional on actually
  doing due diligence, not just removing a listing after getting caught) applies to
  every marketplace and listings app readers use daily.
- **Real anchor:** Central Consumer Protection Authority order against Just Dial Ltd,
  dated 28 October 2026 — ₹10 lakh penalty for facilitating ammonium nitrate listings
  without verifying the seller's regulated-product compliance, while earning
  subscription/campaign revenue from those same listings. The CCPA rejected Just Dial's
  Section 79 IT Act "intermediary safe harbour" defense (which cited *MySpace Inc. v.
  Super Cassettes*), holding that safe harbour depends on demonstrated due diligence,
  not just post-hoc removal. The order cites Just Dial's own scale (~54.7 million
  listings, ~192.9 million quarterly unique users). Same CCPA action against seven
  platforms overall delisted ~3,940 sellers/listings and levied ~₹30 lakh in combined
  penalties. Reported by LiveLaw, The Week, Taxguru, Inc42 and digitalpolicyalert.org
  (independent corroboration across outlets, order date consistent across all).
- **Angle source:** General India news/regulatory scan (BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5),
  not a duplicate of any post in the last ~2 weeks of this log or the known archive
  (no prior JustDial/CCPA/safe-harbour post found).

## Topic 2: Why That 24-Hour Wait Before You Can Pay a New UPI Contact Actually Exists — RBI Just Fined a Wallet App for Skipping It

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE
- **Pattern:** 1 — "why does this everyday friction happen"
- **The hook:** Every wallet/UPI app makes you wait before you can send money to someone
  you haven't paid before, and it's mildly annoying every single time. That cooling-off
  window — a "non-pre-registered beneficiary" limit, in regulator-speak — exists
  specifically to slow down exactly the kind of transfer that scammers rely on. RBI
  just fined a payments company for letting some of those transfers through anyway, and
  for sitting on fraud reports past the deadline it owed those same users.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Dated this month. The fine amount (₹9 lakh) looks
  trivial next to a company with lakhs of active users — the "why now" is precisely that
  gap between the size of the penalty and the size of the safety feature it was
  attached to; the post can use that mismatch as its own hook rather than overstating
  the fine's severity.
- **Real anchor:** RBI order dated 6 November 2026 — ₹9 lakh monetary penalty on One
  MobiKwik Systems Limited under Section 30(1) read with Section 26(6) of the Payment
  and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, for (a) allowing fund transfers by certain PPI
  (prepaid payment instrument) holders to non-pre-registered beneficiaries beyond the
  prescribed limit, and (b) failing to report certain frauds within the prescribed
  timeline. Reported via RBI's own press release (prid 63973) and corroborated by
  Inc42 and Studycafe.
- **Angle source:** General India news/regulatory scan (BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §5). No
  MobiKwik/PPI-beneficiary-limit post found in the last ~2 weeks or in the known
  archive; distinct from the bank-fee and SEBI-fine posts already published this month.
