## Topic 1: Why Do We Keep Chasing the "IPO Listing Pop" Even Though It's Been Shrinking All Year

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** "Why do WE [universal behavior]" (#2), anchored to a same-day
  dated news hook — the psychology is the actual story, the NSE listing is
  the trigger.
- **The hook:** Today, September 24, 2026, the National Stock Exchange
  itself — India's second-biggest IPO ever at ₹22,562 crore — lists on the
  BSE. It was oversubscribed 5.71 times. And yet its grey-market premium
  collapsed from ₹310 before the offer opened to just ₹68 on listing eve,
  pointing to a listing-day gain of roughly 2-4% instead of the double-digit
  "pop" retail investors have come to expect. This isn't one unlucky IPO —
  it's this year's pattern. Why do we keep piling into oversubscribed IPOs
  expecting a payday that the data says is disappearing?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** It's genuinely two stories at once,
  matching the site's "full picture" tagline: a same-day breaking market
  event (NSE's own listing) plus a documented, quantified year-long trend
  behind it, not just vibes. It also lets the piece do what the site does
  well — take a raw statistic and turn it into "here's the mechanism in
  your own head," rather than a dry market wrap-up.
- **Real anchor:** Two linked, independently reported data points: (1) NSE's
  own IPO — priced at ₹1,785/share, subscribed 5.71x, ₹90,300 crore in total
  demand — saw its grey-market premium fall from ₹310 pre-launch to ₹68 on
  the eve of its September 24, 2026 listing (HDFC Sky, NewsBytesApp,
  AnalyticsInsight, Business Standard), implying a listing-day gain near
  2-4% versus the double-digit premiums investors bid for oversubscription
  on. (2) BusinessToday (Aug 10, 2026), citing a primary-market readiness
  report: FY26 saw a record ₹1.77 lakh crore raised across 109 mainboard
  IPOs, but average listing-day gains fell from 29% in FY25 to just 7% in
  FY26, while average oversubscription fell from 71x to 39x over the same
  period — a documented cooling, not a one-off. Writer should pull the
  actual post-listing closing price for NSE (not yet available at scan time,
  since the stock lists today) before publishing, and cite the primary
  readiness-index report directly rather than the aggregator summary.

## Topic 2: Why a Court-Ordered Audit Was Needed to Find Out Where the Ram Temple's Donations Actually Went

- **Category:** POLITICS / BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** India news/scandal explained (#4) — fact-dense, explicit
  sourcing, explained rather than argued, same family as the site's
  Harshad-Mehta-comparison and JPSC-JSSC exam-row pieces.
- **The hook:** The Ram Janmbhoomi Teerth Kshetra trust manages one of the
  largest donation flows of any religious institution in India. A
  Supreme-Court-monitored Special Investigation Team has now spent months
  going through its CCTV footage, cash logs and audit records — and found
  105 separate instances of cash being withdrawn without authorisation. The
  SIT is legally required to file its first chargesheet by September 25,
  2026, or the accused get default bail. How does an institution handling
  crores in public donations end up needing a police forensic audit just to
  answer "where did the cash go"?
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is a hard, court-imposed deadline
  (September 25, 2026 — tomorrow) with a Supreme Court bench (CJI Surya
  Kant) actively supervising the case, so it's live and dated rather than a
  retrospective. It's also a rare story that lets the site do "explained,
  not argued" on something genuinely sensitive — the piece should stick
  strictly to the SIT's own documented findings and the court record, not
  political claims about the amount involved (which range wildly, from ~₹7
  crore per the initial forensic estimate to ₹200 crore per unverified
  political statements — the gap between those two numbers is itself part
  of the story, and the piece should flag it as unresolved rather than
  picking one).
- **Real anchor:** The Supreme Court-monitored SIT probe into alleged
  embezzlement of Ram Temple (Ayodhya) donations — CCTV-based forensic audit
  identified 105 instances of unauthorised cash withdrawal; the SIT was
  told by the Solicitor General that the statutory 90-day chargesheet
  deadline from the first arrest falls on September 25, 2026; a Supreme
  Court bench of CJI Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V
  Mohana directed the chargesheet be filed with the jurisdictional
  additional sessions judge by that date. Reported by The Tribune, LatestLY,
  OpIndia, Deccan Herald and IndiaNewsNetwork (Sept 20-23, 2026). Estimates
  of the amount misappropriated range from ₹7-7.5 crore (initial forensic
  estimate) to ₹200 crore (political statements, unverified) — writer must
  keep these clearly separated as FACT vs UNVERIFIED CLAIM per the repo's
  own ground rules, pull the SC order and SIT status report directly, and
  should not imply a final finding of guilt ahead of the chargesheet/trial.
