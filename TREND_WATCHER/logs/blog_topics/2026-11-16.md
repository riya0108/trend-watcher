## Topic 1: Why SEBI Just Ordered a "Trading Guru" to Return ₹546 Crore — and Banned Him for Two Years

- **Category:** FINANCE
- **Pattern:** Breaking India news/scandal, explained (shape 4)
- **The hook:** A trading-education academy that trained hundreds of thousands of retail investors just got hit with one of SEBI's largest-ever disgorgement orders — for running what was, legally, an unregistered investment advisory business disguised as "courses."
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** SEBI's final order (passed by quasi-judicial authority N. Murugan) is dated 23 October 2026 and is still actively unfolding — the academy has said it will challenge the order at the Securities Appellate Tribunal, and the refund-notice process to investors is ongoing on a statutory clock (newspaper notices within 30 days, refunds within 5 months of the order). It hasn't had a mainstream "explainer" treatment yet, and it's a clean teaching moment on the line between "financial education" and unregistered advisory — a line a lot of readers cross unknowingly as creators themselves.
- **Real anchor:** SEBI order against Avadhut Sathe Training Academy Pvt Ltd and founder Avadhut Dinkar Sathe (and Gouri Sathe), directing a disgorgement/refund of ~₹546.17 crore to investors, ₹11.5 crore in penalties (₹6 cr academy, ₹4 cr Sathe, ₹1.5 cr Gouri Sathe), and market bars (2 years for the academy/Sathe, 6 months for Gouri Sathe) for providing unregistered investment advice between July 2017 and October 2025. Reported by Outlook Business, LiveLaw, Business Standard, Free Press Journal, exchange4media, Millennium Post (late Oct–Nov 2026); SAT has reportedly granted interim relief letting the academy keep operating pending appeal.
- **Angle source:** General trend/news scan (SEBI enforcement coverage) — not from a specific BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md voice.

## Topic 2: Why Delhi Just Cut GST on Your Delivery Fee to 5% — and You Probably Won't See a Rupee of It

- **Category:** MONEY
- **Pattern:** Dated India money explainer (shape 3), with an everyday-friction "why" hook (shape 1)
- **The hook:** The government just slashed the GST rate on e-commerce delivery services from 18% to 5% — which sounds like delivery fees on Swiggy/Blinkit/Amazon-style apps should drop. The catch buried in the same notification (no input tax credit allowed on that 5%) is exactly the kind of rule-that-helps-on-paper the blog's "Direct Plan Penalty" post (Nov 1) was built on, and nobody's connected it to what it actually means for the price the reader pays.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Took effect 1 November 2026 — it's live right now, mid-pass-through-the-system timing, and has had almost no plain-language consumer coverage; all the reporting so far is tax-trade-press, not reader-facing.
- **Real anchor:** CBIC Notification No. 06/2026-Central Tax (Rate), with companion IGST/UTGST notifications (incl. No. 09/2026-Integrated Tax (Rate)), all dated 27 October 2026, effective 1 November 2026 — setting GST on delivery services supplied through e-commerce operators (other than postal/courier) at 5% without input tax credit, with the e-commerce operator liable to pay under Section 9(5) for delivery partners not required to register. Reported by Business Today, Free Press Journal, TaxGuru, thetaxcorp.in, calcguru.in.
- **Angle source:** General trend/news scan (GST notification coverage) — not from a specific BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md voice.
