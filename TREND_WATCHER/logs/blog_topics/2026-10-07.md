## Topic 1: Why Your Home Loan Just Got More Expensive for the First Time Since Early 2023 — and Why the RBI Wanted It To

- **Category:** PERSONAL FINANCE / MONEY
- **Pattern:** Dated India money explainer (#3) crossed with breaking
  India news explained (#4) — the reader's own floating-rate EMI ticking up
  is the hook, today's actual policy announcement and its stated reasoning
  are the mechanism.
- **The hook:** Today, October 7, 2026, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra
  announced a 25-basis-point hike in the repo rate to 5.50% — the first
  rate hike since February 2023 — and shifted the policy stance to
  "calibrated tightening." If your home loan is on a floating/repo-linked
  rate, your EMI is about to rise for the first time in three and a half
  years. The RBI isn't apologizing for it — raising your borrowing cost is
  explicitly the point this time.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** This is the single biggest, freshest
  financial news story in the country at scan time — hours old, not days.
  It also has the site's trademark "full picture" shape: the RBI isn't
  hiking because the economy is overheating in a good way, but to defend a
  weakening rupee and contain inflation that's broadened across the
  consumption basket, which is a very different (and more worrying) story
  than "rates went up." It directly affects anyone with a floating-rate
  home, car, or personal loan, and flips the savers-vs-borrowers trade-off
  that the site's PPF/FD-rate pieces have already been tracking this
  quarter.
- **Real anchor:** RBI Monetary Policy Committee decision, announced October
  7, 2026 by Governor Sanjay Malhotra: repo rate hiked 25 bps to 5.50% (from
  5.25%, where it had been held since the prior meeting), the first increase
  since February 2023, with the policy stance changed to "calibrated
  tightening" (reported same-day by Upstox, BusinessToday, and other
  financial outlets). BusinessToday's own worked example: on a ₹50 lakh,
  25-year home loan at 7.5%, a 25-bps rise pushes the EMI from ~₹36,950 to
  ~₹37,767/month (~₹817 more/month, ~₹9,800/year, ~₹2.45 lakh in added
  interest over the full tenure, assuming tenure stays fixed rather than the
  bank extending it). Stated/reported drivers: CPI inflation at 4.82% in
  August 2026 with price pressure broadening (roughly half of the inflation
  basket's items now rising 4%+ annually, versus about a third in March); the
  rupee down roughly 6% against the dollar in 2026; and elevated crude oil
  tied to shipping disruption from the Iran-US/Israel conflict near the
  Strait of Hormuz (India imports roughly 85% of the crude it uses).
  **Caveat for the writer:** the rate-hike figure itself (5.25% → 5.50%,
  first since Feb 2023, "calibrated tightening") is well-corroborated across
  multiple financial outlets reporting same-day; the CPI/rupee/oil context
  figures in this paragraph came via aggregator-site search summaries in
  this pass and should be independently verified against RBI's own MPC
  resolution/statement or a primary wire source (Reuters/PTI) before
  publishing, rather than cited solely from this pass's research.

## Topic 2: Why It Took an RTI Request to Find Out Whether Your Teacher's College Was Ever Actually Inspected

- **Category:** POLITICS / LIFESTYLE
- **Pattern:** Breaking India news/scandal explained (#4) — a civil-society
  RTI finding is the news trigger, a second, larger parallel scandal in the
  same state's higher-education system is the "this is a pattern, not a
  one-off" proof.
- **The hook:** Tamil Nadu has roughly 590 B.Ed colleges — the institutions
  that train the state's school teachers — 526 of them private. Civil
  society group Arappor Iyakkam filed an RTI request and found that for 74
  of these colleges granted affiliation by the Tamil Nadu Teachers Education
  University between 2023 and 2025, the official inspection records don't
  add up: the same inspection team is logged as having physically visited
  colleges in different districts on the same day, and some colleges
  appear to have reused the same named faculty to pad out the staffing
  numbers required for affiliation. Nobody would know any of this if a
  watchdog group hadn't had to go digging for the paperwork.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** Reported in the first days of October
  2026 — fresh at scan time. The "full picture" angle is that this isn't an
  isolated complaint: Tamil Nadu is already mid-scandal over near-identical
  "ghost faculty" findings at Anna University's engineering colleges (295
  colleges issued show-cause notices in July 2026 over 353 faculty members
  found listed as full-time staff at multiple colleges simultaneously — two
  professors turned up on the books of 11 different colleges each). Same
  mechanism, same state, two different arms of the higher-education
  affiliation system — which is a more interesting story than either
  scandal alone: a systemic incentive to fake compliance on paper, not a
  one-off bad actor.
- **Real anchor:** Arappor Iyakkam's RTI findings on TNTEU B.Ed college
  affiliation inspections (reported by DT Next, early October 2026): records
  for 74 colleges that applied for affiliation in 2023-2025 show scheduling
  discrepancies, including TNTEU's own registrar listed as personally
  inspecting colleges in Thoothukudi, Salem, and Karur — different
  districts — on the same day (March 28, 2023); Tamil Nadu has ~590 B.Ed
  colleges, ~526 private, per Arappor Iyakkam's own figures. Separately, as
  corroborating pattern evidence (not the same colleges or regulator):
  Anna University issued show-cause notices to 295 affiliated engineering
  colleges on July 27, 2026 (reported by The News Minute, Careers360) over
  353 teachers listed as full-time faculty at more than one college, with
  two professors appearing at 11 different colleges each and three at 10
  each. **Caveat for the writer:** pull the DT Next report and Arappor
  Iyakkam's own RTI documents/press statement directly; "ghost faculty" is
  Arappor Iyakkam's characterization of the discrepancy, not yet an
  adjudicated finding by TNTEU, the NCTE, or a court — frame it as an
  allegation under scrutiny, and keep the BEd/TNTEU numbers (74 colleges)
  and the separate Anna University engineering-college numbers (295
  colleges, 353 teachers) clearly distinct rather than merged into one
  figure.
- **Angle source:** General trend scan (WebSearch) — BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md
  §5.
