## Topic 1: Why Small Online Sellers Have Spent Years Paying for Fake Addresses Just to Sell Outside Their Home State

- **Category:** BUSINESS
- **Pattern:** Shape 3 — dated India money/business explainer, running on Shape 1's "everyday friction" engine (the workaround sellers quietly used, now made obsolete by a rule).
- **The hook:** A seller running a one-person online store in, say, Jaipur who wants to list on Flipkart/Meesho/Amazon and ship from a warehouse in another state has had exactly two legal options: register for GST in every state they want to "have a presence" in (which usually meant paying a CA to arrange a nominal rent agreement for an address they'd never visit), or stay regional. From November 1, 2026, they can instead name the e-commerce platform's own warehouse as their registered place of business in that state — no fake address needed.
- **Why it's underrated / why now:** The rule (new Rule 14B of the CGST Rules) took effect four days before this pass and has only been covered by tax-compliance trade blogs — exactly the kind of "nobody will explain why this matters to an actual small seller" gap Bull or Bear fills.
- **Real anchor:** Central Goods and Services Tax (Amendment) Rules, 2026 insert Rule 14B, following the 57th GST Council Meeting (8 October 2026) recommendation; notified ~26 October 2026, effective 1 November 2026. Confirmed across multiple independent trade/compliance outlets (taxscan.in, a2ztaxcorp.net, thegstco.com, freepressjournal.in) — consistent on the date, the rule number and the mechanism (declaring an e-commerce operator's warehouse as principal place of business).
- **Angle source:** BLOG_TOPICS_SOURCES.md §1 (own site's Business/Finance beat) + general GST-council trade-press coverage surfaced in the discovery scan.
