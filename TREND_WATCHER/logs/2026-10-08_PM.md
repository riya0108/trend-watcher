### New this pass — full draft: The SIR voter-deletion controversy

This topic has enough shape for a full debate piece: multiple confirmed
facts and named positions on both sides.

**Format:** Instagram carousel (multi-layered numbers dispute suits a
carousel better than a Reel's single narrative arc).
**Story broke:** ongoing since early-mid 2026; today's escalation Oct 8;
draft-to-final roll window closes Nov 4, 2026.
**Publish-by deadline:** treat as a slower explainer given the saturated
timing (per PROCESS.md Stage 2C), but with urgency attached to the Nov 4
final-roll date — the "check your name" CTA loses its value after that.

**Truth layer:**
- **FACT:** The Election Commission conducted a Special Intensive Revision
  (SIR) of electoral rolls across multiple states and union territories in
  2026; a draft roll was published; the final roll is due November 4,
  2026.
- **FACT:** In Delhi specifically, 47.56 lakh voters — 32.78% of the
  electorate — were shown as deleted from the draft roll after SIR claims
  were processed through Sept 30 (per ThePrint's reporting of the CEO's
  figures).
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** The Election Commission says its own draft-stage
  figure of roughly 13 crore flagged electors breaks down as ~2.8 crore
  deceased, ~3 crore absent/untraceable, ~6.3 crore permanently shifted,
  and ~1 crore registered at multiple locations (per Morung Express's
  reporting of the ECI's breakdown) — and that this is not the same as a
  final deletion count.
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** Congress MP Jairam Ramesh claims 6.18 crore voters
  out of 36.06 crore electors covered in the ongoing third SIR phase (12
  states and 2 UTs) have been deleted.
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** AAP MP Sanjay Singh alleged 1.37 crore names were
  deleted from Delhi's rolls "in the name of SIR"; AAP's Atishi has
  separately accused the central government directly of removing names.
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan dismissed the
  opposition's objections, saying "if they win, democracy zindabad; if
  they lose, Election Commission murdabad."
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** Per reporting (ETV Bharat / Outlook), 130 Lok Sabha
  MPs and 63 Rajya Sabha MPs have signed a notice seeking CEC Gyanesh
  Kumar's removal, citing alleged partisan conduct and "mass
  disenfranchisement" among seven charges — reportedly the first such
  notice ever moved against a CEC. Not independently verified by this
  agent beyond the cited reporting.
- **INTERPRETATION:** The gap between the ECI's ~13 crore flagged-at-draft
  figure and its own ~2.8 crore confirmed-deceased subset is the real
  battleground — the dispute is less about whether SIR happened and more
  about whether the "absent/shifted/duplicate" categories were applied
  accurately.
- **PREDICTION:** None stated as fact — opposition leaders' reported plan
  to raise this with President Droupadi Murmu in the second week of
  October, and the Oct 10 "Delhi Chalo" march, are plans/announcements, not
  yet events that have occurred as of this pass.
- **UNKNOWN:** What happened at today's (Oct 8) protest; whether the Oct 6
  ECI march took place as planned and with what outcome; how many
  objections against draft-roll deletions will actually be resolved before
  the Nov 4 final roll; a claim (unverified by Indian sources, sourced only
  to Radio Pakistan) that over 700 protesters were detained at an Oct 2
  Delhi protest — flagged explicitly as unverified and from a state
  broadcaster of a non-neutral source, not carried as fact anywhere else in
  this draft.

**The debate:**
- **Side A (bull / for the exercise):** SIR is overdue list hygiene — a
  voter roll that still carries millions of dead, duplicate, or
  long-moved-away names is a bigger democratic risk than a rigorous
  cleanup. The ECI's own breakdown shows most flagged names fall into
  categories (deceased, shifted, duplicate) nobody disputes should be
  removed, and genuine objections can still be filed before Nov 4.
- **Side B (bear / against the exercise):** The scale and opacity of the
  process — millions flagged with reportedly inconsistent criteria, no
  individual notice process clear enough for someone to confirm removal
  before the list locks, and even high-profile political figures
  reportedly caught in the net — means errors at this scale amount to
  disenfranchisement, regardless of intent, for voters least able to
  navigate a correction process in time.
- **Why both sides are real:** Both cite the ECI's own published numbers,
  not just rhetoric — the disagreement is over what those numbers mean and
  whether the correction process is adequate, not over whether SIR
  happened at all.

**So-what:** If your name — or a family member's — was wrongly dropped
from the draft roll, the practical deadline to fix it is before November
4. This isn't an abstract institutional fight; it's a concrete, time-boxed
action item for anyone who hasn't checked.

**Structure sketch:**
1. Hook: "Can you confirm your name is still on the voter list? Most people
   can't — and the final list locks November 4."
2. Facts as established: SIR process, Delhi's 32.78% draft deletion rate,
   the ECI's own category breakdown.
3. Side A, stated fairly: list hygiene case.
4. Side B, stated fairly: disenfranchisement-risk case.
5. So-what: how to actually check your name on the draft roll, and the
   Nov 4 deadline.
6. CTA: invite the audience's own take — don't tell them the answer.
