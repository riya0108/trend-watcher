## Draft — Full debate piece: UPI merchant fee deferral

See `templates/full_debate_piece_template.md` structure below.

**Format:** Carousel (multi-step: what changed, who's exempt, why
deferred again, what's actually being debated)
**Story broke:** Oct 13, ~14:00 IST
**Publish-by deadline:** Oct 14-15 (24-48h from break)

**Truth layer:**
- **FACT:** NPCI's UPI & Services Steering Committee decided on Oct 13 to
  defer the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) rollout on select UPI
  person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions, originally due Oct 15. No new
  implementation date has been announced; the decision will be made after
  Diwali.
- **FACT:** As announced before the deferral, the framework covers a 0.4%
  MDR on P2M UPI transactions above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300; peer-to-peer
  transfers and small merchants using existing QR codes (up to ~₹1 lakh/
  month, per earlier reporting) remain exempt.
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** Trader associations and fintech/payment companies
  publicly asked NPCI to delay the rollout, citing the festive shopping
  season and concern that merchants might refuse UPI for larger purchases
  or pass the cost to customers (per Telangana Today, Business Today,
  Inc42 reporting from Oct 8).
  - NPCI itself estimates the fee would affect roughly 4% of merchant
    transactions (per reported figures).
- **INTERPRETATION:** This is at least the second deferral of a UPI
  merchant fee in 2026, which suggests the underlying disagreement — who
  bears the cost of UPI's payment-processing infrastructure — remains
  unresolved rather than settled by delay.
- **PREDICTION (not fact):** Some reports (Inc42, KNN India) suggest the
  rollout could be pushed as far as January 2027 — this is reported
  speculation, not a confirmed NPCI date.
- **UNKNOWN:** The actual new implementation date; whether the final
  framework will differ from what was announced for Oct 15; whether
  merchants above the threshold will in practice pass the cost to
  consumers once it does take effect.

**The debate:**
- **Side A (fee is necessary):** Payment rails cost money to run. A
  system marketed as permanently free to both payer and payee isn't
  actually free anywhere in the chain — someone (banks, NPCI, or
  taxpayers via subsidy) is absorbing the processing cost today. A modest,
  threshold-gated fee on larger merchant transactions is a reasonable way
  to make the system self-sustaining without touching ordinary P2P use or
  small vendors.
- **Side B (timing and burden are wrong):** Introducing a new merchant
  cost right before the country's biggest shopping season risks pushing
  small and mid-size merchants back toward cash or into absorbing a cost
  increase during their highest-volume weeks — exactly when digital
  adoption should be reinforced, not taxed.
- **Why both sides are real:** This isn't a "obviously wrong side" dispute
  — it's a genuine timing-and-burden-sharing disagreement between
  payments-infrastructure economics and small-merchant cash flow, and
  NPCI's own repeated deferrals show the tension hasn't actually been
  resolved internally either.

**So-what:** If you run a shop, stall, or small business that takes UPI
for anything over ₹2,000, this fee — whenever it lands — changes your
margins. If you're a customer, watch whether merchants start setting a
"cash preferred above ₹2,000" line once it does take effect.

**Structure sketch:**
1. Hook: "UPI just avoided a fee change for the second time this year."
2. Facts: what the Oct 15 rule would have done, who's exempt, what
   happened Oct 13.
3. Side A: the processing-cost argument.
4. Side B: the festive-timing/small-merchant argument.
5. So-what: what to watch once a new date is set.
6. CTA: ask the audience whether merchants should ever charge for UPI.
