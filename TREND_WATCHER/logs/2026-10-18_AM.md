### Full debate piece draft — RBI repo rate hike to 5.50%

**Format:** Instagram carousel (multi-step argument — timeline, numbers,
two sides — suits carousel better than Reel).
**Story broke:** Oct 7, 2026 (decision); Oct 21, 2026 (minutes, forward
peg).
**Publish-by deadline:** Oct 21-22, 2026.

**Truth layer:**
- **FACT:** RBI's MPC raised the repo rate by 25bps to 5.50% on Oct 7,
  2026 — the first hike since Feb 2023. Policy stance changed from
  "neutral" to "calibrated tightening." Standing Deposit Facility rate now
  5.25%; Marginal Standing Facility rate and Bank Rate now 5.75%. RBI
  raised its FY27 CPI inflation projection to 5.2% and its FY27 real GDP
  growth forecast to 7.1% (from 6.7%); Q1 FY27 GDP grew 7.8%. MPC minutes
  are due Oct 21, 2026.
- **ATTRIBUTED CLAIM:** Multiple financial-press sources report the
  rate-hike vote itself was unanimous, but say two MPC members — Dr.
  Nagesh Kumar and Prof. Ram Singh — preferred to retain the neutral
  stance rather than shift to calibrated tightening (sources are not
  fully reconciled on the exact vote count on the stance question — flag
  as attributed, pending the official Oct 21 minutes). RBI Governor Sanjay
  Malhotra is reported as saying a rate cut is off the table in the near
  term. An economist quoted by Outlook Money estimates that on a ₹50 lakh,
  20-year home loan at 7.5%, this specific 25bps hike could raise the EMI
  by roughly ₹770/month, and expects banks to pass the hike through within
  a quarter since most home loans are linked to external benchmarks.
  Brickwork Ratings is reported to have called this a "pre-emptive" hike.
- **INTERPRETATION:** A hike alongside an *upgraded* growth forecast reads
  as RBI betting it can tighten without choking momentum — i.e., acting
  on inflation risk ahead of it showing up in the data, not in reaction to
  a current spike.
- **OPINION:** (left for the human writer — e.g., "protecting purchasing
  power now beats a bigger hike later" vs. "borrowers are being asked to
  front-run a risk that hasn't materialized yet" — not asserted by this
  agent).
- **PREDICTION:** RBI's own forecast that inflation will average ~5.8%
  over the next three quarters and peak near 6.0% in Q3 FY27 is RBI's
  projection, not a settled fact. The "banks will reprice within a
  quarter" estimate is likewise a forecast, not yet observed.
- **UNKNOWN:** The exact MPC vote split on the stance change (sources
  disagree on the mechanics; the Oct 21 minutes should resolve this). How
  quickly and fully individual banks actually reprice existing floating
  loans in practice.

**The debate:**
- **Side A (bull / the hike is right):** Pre-emptive tightening now avoids
  a sharper, more painful hike later if inflation does hit the 6% RBI is
  flagging; keeps India's rate differential attractive to foreign capital
  — relevant timing given this is the same week Jio Platforms' IPO is
  trying to pull in >$20B of anchor-investor money (see Candidate 2).
- **Side B (bear / premature or wrongly timed):** GDP is already running
  hot (7.8% in Q1, forecast revised up) — cooling policy into an upgraded
  growth story risks choking it unnecessarily; the hike lands directly on
  existing floating-rate borrowers' EMIs now, for a risk that is still a
  forecast; two of the RBI's own rate-setters publicly didn't back the
  tightening stance, undercutting the "committee is unified" framing.
- Why both sides are real: the MPC's own reported stance-vote split is
  direct evidence of internal disagreement — not a strawman where one side
  is obviously wrong.

**So-what:** A 25bps hike is not abstract — it's a recurring, automatic
repricing for anyone on an external-benchmark-linked floating loan
(home/auto/personal), landing as a real monthly number in the weeks
after Oct 7, with banks reportedly expected to finish passing it through
within the quarter.
